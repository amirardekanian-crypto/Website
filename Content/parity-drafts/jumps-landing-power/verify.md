## (c) LESSON FIXES (the TPS lesson and its English twin)
1. **Jump-dose line is overstated.** Lesson says "small studies on children and teenagers ... about 50 to 60 jumps". The source (Johnson 2011, PMID 21849911) is 7 low-quality studies on **prepubertal children aged 5-14**. Change to that, and keep "low quality".
2. **Serve line is mis-sourced.** "Throws next to a full strength programme can make serve a little faster" credits Canos 2022, which did not test throws. Honest version: throws alone often did not change shot speed (Martin-Miguel 2025, post-pubertal and adult players); programmes that mix leg jumps and upper-body jump or throw work did improve serve speed in juniors (Fernandez-Fernandez 2015; Hu 2026; Zhou 2025). Also add that Martin-Miguel's sample was post-pubertal and adult, not juniors.
3. **The 60-contact cap, the 4/3 run caps, the 15 cm hurdle, 20/30 cm drop heights and the under-16 "no depth jumps, no weighted jumps" are practical rules, not trial-based.** The lesson sentence "Under 16: 60 jumps a session at most" reads as a research limit. Label it as the programme's rule. I could not confirm a published contact ceiling or an explicit depth-jump ban in Lloyd 2014 (abstract only, no open full text), so the notes must not cite Lloyd 2014 for a number unless Amir has the paper.
4. Lesson says "no depth jumps for anyone, they are for level 3 and from age 16": fine as a programme rule, but nothing verified says age 16 is the cut-off. Treat as practical.
5. Add (optional): jump training has no padel-specific trial; the lesson and the course are for tennis and padel, so say "tennis data, padel is the same movement logic".
6. The lesson's reference to growth spurts is a precaution, not a proven effect: Moran 2017 shows a smaller jump gain at about 13-16 years in boys, nothing more.
7. Course-specific wording removed in the article (Club A, Court B, Blocks 3 and 4, week-by-week path, "16 weeks", "the card", lighter weeks 4/8/12/16, "Amir puts them in your programme").
