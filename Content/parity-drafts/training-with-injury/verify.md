## (c) LESSON FIXES (what the TPS lessons must change)
The new lesson is written to avoid these. They are about the lessons that already exist:
1. **`pain-red-flags`** (learn-4) gives the athlete pain thresholds ("3 or less, carry on", "4 or 5, change the movement", "2 or less on the easier version") and the sentence "the decision is yours" for any pain. Those numbers are practice-based. The tested pain-monitoring rule exists only for Achilles tendinopathy and patellofemoral pain in adults and young adults. For a doctor-diagnosed injury the clinician's rule must win. Suggest one added line in that lesson: "If a doctor or physio has already seen this injury, follow their rule and the lesson «تمرین با آسیب‌دیدگی و برگشت بعد از آن»." The English twin gets the same line.
2. **`pain-red-flags`**, the "before the next session" row "mild (1 to 3), first time or improving: train" is for new minor soreness. Without a clinician's rule it should not read as permission for a diagnosed injury. Same pointer as above.
3. **`missed-sessions`**, section "return after injury": add a pointer to the new lesson. Its content ("only when the doctor says full, no restrictions; yes with limits means their plan for that area") agrees with the new lesson, so nothing there is wrong.
4. Note for the course owner: the new lesson says the card's easier version is used only where the clinician allows it. That is the product rule (Amir 2026-09-14), but `pain-red-flags` lets the athlete choose the easier version on their own pain judgement. That is fine for soreness, so the two lessons stay consistent only if the new lesson's scope (a diagnosed injury) is kept clear.

## (d) Could not verify
- The 5/10 and "settled by next morning" thresholds of the pain-monitoring model: not in the PubMed abstracts. Confirmed only from web summaries of the model, full texts not read.
- Silbernagel 2007 and Thomee 1997 were small trials (38 and 40 people) and about recovery protocols, not about the safety of a rule in general. The article says so by limiting it to two problems.
- AMSSM overuse statement (24463910) and PEACE and LOVE (31377722): abstracts not available. Used only for a qualitative sentence (AMSSM) or not at all (PEACE and LOVE).
- "Some tears, like a complete ACL tear, need a surgeon's opinion": clinical knowledge, no source read.
- No tennis- or padel-specific return-from-injury study was found. The article says the research is general sports medicine.
- The cohort result in Grindem 2016 is observational and one cohort of 106 players.

Also the lesson's emergency number 115 and the Farsi «اورژانس».
- The example pain rule "up to about 4/10, back to usual by next morning, not creeping up week to week", presented as something the clinician sets.

# LESSON vs ARTICLE DIFFERENCES

The course lesson (`lesson.fa.json` Farsi, `lesson.en.json` English twin) and the article come from the same evidence but have different jobs.

| Topic | Article (website and Playbook) | Course lesson |
|---|---|---|
| Who decides | Doctor or physio sets the limits, the coach builds the plan inside them, the athlete reports, parents know everything | The **doctor or physio decides**. The course never decides. Every action is "until the clinician has allowed it, don't ...". This follows the product rule: the course does every session as written |
| Sport | Tennis and padel | Tennis only |
| Voice | Colloquial Tehrani (می‌کنه، بذار) and plain English in Amir's voice | Formal-neutral Farsi like the other lessons («شما»، می‌کند), plain English twin |
| Pain rule | Explains the evidence (Achilles and kneecap, adults and young adults), then gives a **sample** rule (about 4/10, settled by morning, not creeping up) that "your clinician sets" | **No number is given.** The athlete follows only the rule the clinician gives. Only the stop signs (sharp, worsening, not settled by morning) are stated, and a pointer to «درد یا کوفتگی؟ و علامت‌های خطر» |
| Easier version | Not mentioned | The card's easier version is used **only where the clinician allows it**. No shortened or lighter session, ever |
| Red flags | Eight-item "see a doctor or emergency" list | The same eight items, plus a pointer to the full lists in «درد یا کوفتگی؟ و علامت‌های خطر», the warn box and 115. Not duplicated beyond the injury-specific ones |
| Scans | Gives the prevalence figures (37% disc, about 4% meniscus) and the heal-or-manage question | No numbers. "A doctor reads the scan", plus the same question to ask |
| Strength reduces injuries, detraining | Included with trial counts and the detraining finding | **Left out.** The lesson has no job of persuading, and "ask what you can still train" would invite the athlete to choose |
| Weaker side first | Included as coaching practice | **Left out.** The card decides what each side does, and the lesson does not add rules to the card |
| ACL cohort (symmetry, waiting up to nine months) | Included, two sentences | Left out (a figure about surgery belongs with the clinician). The lesson keeps only "rushing has a cost" |
| Growing athletes and parents | A paragraph and a four-item list | Same content in its own section, plus the under-18 callout in the course's own style |
| Matches and load | Evidence sentence (IOC) and a four-item list | Same, shortened, with «مربی تنیس» |
| Comeback stages | Five-item list, labelled a common way | Same five items as an `ol`, plus a link to «جلسهٔ ازدست‌رفته و برگشت بعد از وقفه» for where to restart |
