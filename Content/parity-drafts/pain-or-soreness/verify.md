## (c) LESSON FIXES (for the TPS lesson and its English twin)

1. **"Soreness eases within 1 to 3 days" is wrong as written.** Soreness peaks around 48-72 hours after the session, so it can be at its worst on day 2 or 3. Change both language versions to something like "it builds over a day, peaks around 2 to 3 days, then fades over a few days".
2. **Say that the ladder and the day-after rows are practice-based, not from a trial.** The lesson presents them without that statement. Also say that the pain-monitoring research (up to about 5/10, settled by next morning, not rising week to week) covers Achilles tendinopathy and patellofemoral pain in adults and young adults only. The lesson's thresholds are stricter than that evidence, which is fine, but the lesson should not imply they came from a study.
3. **The lesson says a rise of 2 points means "getting worse" and gives other cut-offs, and "after 3 pain-free days train as your programme says".** Both are rules of thumb. Consider "build back up step by step" for the return, as the article does.
4. **Add "breathlessness" check with the heart item, and keep bladder/bowel loss as its own line** (done in the article). In the lesson these were mixed with other text.
5. Possible addition the course notes already raise (not in the article): severe muscle pain with very dark urine after training means medical help the same day (rhabdomyolysis). I did not verify it, so it is not in the article. **Doctor to check** before adding.
6. The lesson's "Amir isn't there mid-session" and "tell Amir in the Coach tab" are course-only. Not carried into the public article.

## (d) Could not verify

- The exact duration of soreness (the "about 5-7 days" end point). I found the 48-72 h peak (Hotfiel 2018) but no abstract stating when it resolves. The article says "fades over a few days".
- The "not creeping up from week to week" clause of the pain-monitoring model. The abstract of Silbernagel 2007 does not state it, and the full text was not available to me. The 5/10 ceiling and the next-morning rule are confirmed from Sprague 2021 (full text, which cites Thomeé and Silbernagel).
- Any evidence that the pain-monitoring model works for tennis or padel injuries, or for under-18s (the patellofemoral trial included ages 15-28, mixed; no tendinopathy trial under 16 was found). Not claimed.
- Sport-specific data for padel at all. The article makes no padel-specific claim.
- A reference that gives numeric thresholds for "limp, swelling, night pain" in young athletes. None cited; all kept qualitative.
- Specifics of the AMSSM youth overuse statement (abstract not available) and the IOC youth statement (abstract has no pain-rule content). Used only for the general point that youth athletes need adults involved.

## (e) Doctor to check

- All Level 1 red flags (chest pain, fainting, racing/irregular heartbeat, unusual breathlessness, head blow with symptoms, bilateral leg numbness/weakness, bladder/bowel change) and the instruction "no training until a doctor has seen you".
- All Level 2 red flags and the instruction to leave the area out and see a doctor or physio (especially "night pain: see a doctor within a few days" and the growing-player rule).
- The 7-day and 14-day persistence rules and the "3 pain-free days, then build back up step by step" rule.
- The statement that the rest of the body may keep training when one area is left out.
- The emergency number «۱۱۵» in the Farsi article (the lesson's own).
- Whether a physio, not only a doctor, should be named as the person to clear a return for the heart and head items (the article names only a doctor for those).
