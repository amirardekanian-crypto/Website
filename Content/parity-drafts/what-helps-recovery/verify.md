# Evidence check: What Actually Helps (`what-helps-recovery`)

Checked 2026-11-24 against each paper's PubMed abstract (PMID and DOI below). Full texts were not read.
Every number in the article sits in these abstracts, apart from the arithmetic and the practice lines listed at the end.
The papers come from the round-14 evidence brief (the Changeover page); a cheaper agent compared each claim with the abstract
text pulled from PubMed, and the wording was then fitted to what the abstract says.

## Left out on purpose

- **2026 network meta-analysis of recovery methods** (PMID 41647155): the record is about elite soccer players, and the 24-hour timing and the wide-interval caveat the Changeover page gave are not in its abstract.
- **2026 protein meta-analysis** (PMID 42625626): the abstract does not say supplements helped only where they added energy.
- **Piñero 2024, cold water and muscle growth:** not found on PubMed or Semantic Scholar (rate limited). Roberts 2015 carries the less-muscle result instead.
- **Stanley 2013, Herbert 2011 (Cochrane), Peake 2017:** read, not needed.

## Practice, not research (labelled "my suggestion" or "my routine" in the text)

- Go to bed a bit earlier after a big day. Think about the day's total, not the clock, when the next hard session is more than 4 hours away.
- Leave 6 hours or more between lifting and hard conditioning (built on Robineau 2016). The app's own gap rule is REC-5, which the article does not restate.
- Cold water after the last match of the day, when you play again tomorrow (the studies looked at training, not tournaments). The heart and blood pressure line is the same wording as the other recovery articles.
- The Turnaround routine between matches, a quiet break for the head, and keeping the first gym session after a long tournament light on legs with heavy squats, sprints and big jumps from day 3 (built on Ojala and Häkkinen 2013 and Thomas 2018).
