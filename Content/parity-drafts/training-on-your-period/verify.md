## (d) Could not verify
- ACOG CO 651 age and day thresholds (paywalled; only the abstract was readable). Kept qualitative-ish with "about" and marked.
- "No study links core exercise to harm in menstruation": my search found none, but this is absence of evidence. Wording stays "no study... I found" style, and the article also says core strengthening has been used as a treatment.
- No ITF or WTA guidance on periods was found. I did not read the ITF/WTA sites in full; only PubMed was searched for tennis or padel with menstrual terms (21 hits, one relevant: Otaka 2018). Padel: nothing found.
- Whether juniors (under 18) differ from the adult samples in Bruinvels 2016/2020 and Pedlar 2018: not studied in those papers. The article says "adult" where it applies.
- Zheng 2024 and the Cochrane review disagree on resistance training (the Cochrane trials used none; Zheng ranks it). Both are reported as stated.

## (e) Doctor to check
- Thresholds: no first period by about 15 or about 3 years after breast development; periods stop 3 months or more; cycles often shorter than about 21 or longer than about 45 days; soaking a pad or tampon every 1-2 hours; bleeding longer than about 7 days.
- "New dizziness, breathlessness or tiredness" with heavy bleeding as a reason to be seen; and the lesson's "same day" wording plus emergency number 115 (lesson warn box).
- Iron: "ask a doctor to check iron, no iron tablets on your own" (and the lesson callout "iron tablets only after a blood test and a doctor's advice").
- Heat pack on the lower belly as a comfort measure for teenagers (standard advice; confirm no objection).
- "Pain that doubles you over, or doesn't ease" as a doctor trigger.
- REDs paragraph and the "tell a parent and see a doctor" line.
- Farsi terms: «انرژی در دسترس پایین», «شکستگی استرسی», «نوار بهداشتی», «کم‌خونی», «پیشگیری هورمونی».
