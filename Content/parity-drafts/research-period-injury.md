# Verified findings for the period and injury articles (research agent, 2026-10-02)

Source of truth for rewriting `training-on-your-period` and `training-with-injury`. PMIDs were confirmed in PubMed (abstracts read).
Where the agent could not read the full text it says so. Use these, re-check any figure you quote.

## Training on your period: claims and verdicts
- Period week is hard for many athletes: SUPPORTED. 6,812 exercising women: 84% reported cramps, 86% tiredness; higher symptom score linked to missing or changing training (Bruinvels 2020, 33199360). 108 elite athletes: 77% of non-pill users reported negative effects, mostly days 1-2 (Martin 2018, 29283683; Findlay 2020, 32349965).
- Cycle-phase programming has weak evidence: SUPPORTED. Meta-analysis: trivial effects (ES -0.06, CrI -0.16 to 0.04), low quality, authors recommend a personalised approach (McNulty 2020, 32661839); umbrella review: premature to say hormones change strength/hypertrophy (Colenso-Semple 2023, 37033884; Blagrove 2020, 32456980; Meignie 2021, 34093223). Wording: "low-quality, inconsistent, small if any, and individual". NOT "no effect".
- Period days as symptom management: SUPPORTED with caveat. Athletes perceive performance as worse in early follicular and late luteal phases, objective tests show no consistent effect (Carmichael 2021, 33572406 narrative review). Not everyone has symptoms: "if you get symptoms".
- "Doesn't need a different program": INCONSISTENT with the different week the article then gives. Say: normal plan, adjusted by how you feel.
- Steady cardio "full duration": PRACTICE-BASED. Cochrane: regular exercise (45-60 min, 3+ times a week, any intensity) cut period pain by about 25 mm on a 100 mm scale, low-quality evidence, over weeks, says nothing about one day (Armour 2019, 31538328). Use "as tolerated". Heavy bleeding or tiredness is a reason to shorten.
- More mobility/stretching: supported but overstated (Armour 2019; Zheng 2024 NMA, 39550537): a coaching choice.
- "Gentle movement increases blood flow and eases cramps; lying down makes it last longer": NOT SUPPORTED as written. Honest: regular exercise lowers period pain on average, many athletes find light movement helps on the day. Heat was as effective as, or better than, painkillers in one meta-analysis (Armour 2019 BMC, 30654775).
- Main lifts down to about 2 sets, lighter weight and RPE: PRACTICE-BASED, no direct evidence. Strength is minimally affected across the cycle (g <= 0.35, Blagrove 2020). Resistance exercise ranked among the best for period pain over weeks (Zheng 2024). Make it symptom-led: train as planned if you feel fine; if you feel bad, trim sets and RPE.
- **"Skip heavy ab-bracing / planks / rollouts outright": NOT SUPPORTED. Remove.** No study links core work to harm during menstruation; core strengthening is itself a studied treatment (Sener 2026, 41877635; Zheng 2024).
- "Doubled over: regress it": sensible, but pain that doubles you over is also a reason to see a doctor.
- "Lighter session that happens beats a normal one skipped out of guilt": practice-based (coaching), no study. Qualitative work: athletes adapt, sometimes conceal symptoms, want better support (Chica-Latorre 2025, 40664584).

## Period article: MUST ADD (juniors and parents safety)
See a doctor for (qualitative first; the numbers come from secondary summaries of ACOG Committee Opinion 651 (26595586, paywalled), so flag "Doctor to check"):
- no first period by about 15, or about 3 years after breast development began;
- periods that stop for 3 months or more after being regular, or cycles regularly shorter than about 21 or longer than about 45 days;
- very heavy bleeding: soaking a pad or tampon every 1-2 hours, bleeding longer than about 7 days, or new dizziness, breathlessness or tiredness;
- pain that doubles you over, makes you miss school or sport, or that usual measures do not ease.
Heavy bleeding and iron: heavy bleeding was reported by 36-54% of athlete samples; about a third reported a history of anaemia; only 22% had sought advice (Bruinvels 2016, 26901873; Pedlar 2018, 29280410): say "get iron checked by a doctor", never self-treat.
Missed periods are NOT a normal sign of being fit: low energy availability (REDs) can cause missed periods, stress fractures, fatigue (IOC 2023, Mountjoy, 37752011). Never praise very lean bodies or weight loss at this age. REDs evidence is mostly associational (Jeukendrup 2024, 39287777).
Medicine and hormonal contraception are for parents and a doctor, not a coach. Tracking symptoms is information for a doctor, not a performance tool. No tennis- or padel-specific menstrual guidance was found (ITF/WTA): the sources are general athlete literature, say so.

## Training with injury: claims and verdicts
- Pain "is information, not a stop sign": supported but overstated. Chronic MSK pain: exercise allowing some pain gave a small short-term benefit (SMD -0.27) and no difference later (Smith 2017, 28596288). Not for acute fractures, complete tears, nerve symptoms, post-surgery. Under-training can raise injury risk (Gabbett 2016, 26758673).
- "Mild, settles by morning = green light; sharp / worsening / still there next day = red flag": supported but narrower. Silbernagel's pain-monitoring model (17307888; 5-year 21084657; Thomee 1997 9413448): pain during exercise up to about 5/10, post-exercise pain allowed if settled by next morning, not rising week to week. Verified only for Achilles tendinopathy and patellofemoral pain, in adults and young adults (15-28). The article's "mild" is stricter, "still there next day = stop" stricter than the model, "sharp" is a clinical add-on. Use: "up to about 4/10, back to usual by the next morning, not creeping up week to week; sharp, worsening or unsettled pain means stop and tell an adult and your coach."
- Scan reports "don't mean much alone": SUPPORTED. 37% of asymptomatic 20-year-olds have disc degeneration; 4% of asymptomatic knees under 40 have meniscal tears; 37% of asymptomatic hips have cam morphology, 54.8% in athletes (Brinjikji 2015, 25430861; Culvenor 2019, 29886437; Frank 2015, 25636988). A clinician reads the scan.
- "A torn structure follows a healing timeline and needs progressive load": overstated. Load drives repair in tendon, muscle, cartilage, bone (Khan & Scott 2009, 19244270; PEACE & LOVE, Dubois 2020, 31377722; tendon continuum, Cook & Purdam 2009, 18812414). But tendinopathy is not a tear, a complete ACL tear does not heal on its own and some meniscus tears do not either (agent's own knowledge, unverified). For ACL, return timing matters: each month of delay up to 9 months cut reinjury by 51% (Grindem 2016, 27162233). Say: "many injured tissues heal over weeks to months and need progressive loading. Ask your clinician which applies to you."
- "Structural trait needs permanent strength work": practice-based. "Weak side sets the pace, stop the strong side at 8, or the gap becomes the next injury": practice-based. Reword as a coaching convention: start on the weaker side and match its reps. Symmetry has support in ACL return (Grindem 2016); cross-education reduced strength loss in immobilisation (g 0.53, Rodriguez-Coloma 2026, 42141765).
- "Your matches are load, tell your coach": SUPPORTED (Soligard 2016, 27535989; Gabbett 2016).
- Five-stage comeback: practice-based, consistent with the consensus that return to sport is a continuum and a risk decision (Ardern 2016, 27226389).
- "None of this depends on your coach getting the programming right, that part isn't yours to carry": RISKY for juniors. Cut it. Parent, coach and clinician share responsibility.
- Add (supported): strength training reduces sports injuries (RR 0.315 in 2014, 24100287; RR 0.338 in the 2018 update, 30131332, 6 trials ages 12-40); stretching alone showed no benefit. Detraining: under 4 weeks VO2max falls quickly, strength only a little; fitness can be kept with reduced volume if intensity is kept (Mujika 2000, 10966148 and 10999420).

## Injury article: MUST ADD
Go to a doctor or emergency care for: cannot put weight on the limb, visible deformity, a locked or giving-way joint, numbness or weakness, fever with a swollen joint, pain at rest or at night, pain getting worse over days, swelling after a fall or hit (standard clinical practice, flag "Doctor to check"). Growing athletes are not small adults (growth-plate pain at heel, knee, elbow; stress fractures): no self-managed pain rule without an adult and a clinician (IOC youth development consensus, 26084524; AMSSM overuse, 24463910). Tell parents about all load (school sport, extra matches). Scans in juniors are read by a doctor.
