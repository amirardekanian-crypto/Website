## (c) LESSON FIXES (TPS lesson `learn-2/strength` and its English twin in `course_en`)

1. **Load-stall sentence is overstated.** The lesson says, in the "How it progresses" section, that in a junior tennis study "progress stopped after a few weeks when the weight stayed the same" (`_notes`: "the load-stall line = Canos 2022"). The abstract (PMID 35959344) says nothing about the weight staying the same. It reports gains in jump, 10 m sprint and agility by week 4 that did not continue to week 8 in 24 junior players on machine or flywheel training, and the authors point to "little exercise variability or load management". Fix: "In one small 8-week study of young tennis players, gains by week 4 didn't continue to week 8. The authors pointed to little variety and little load management." Correct the `_notes` entry too.
2. **Serve-speed source.** `_notes` credits the "modestly improve serve speed" line to Canos 2022, but that abstract reports no serve-speed result. Replace with Deng 2025 (PMID 39839526: 16 RCTs, resistance ES 0.53, multimodal ES 0.79) and Hu 2026 (PMID 42730140). The lesson's "small studies" wording is fine, and "can" is right because Martin-Miguel 2025 (PMID 39976396) found no change from some strength-endurance and medicine ball work in adults.
3. **Add the RIR accuracy limit.** The RPE section teaches "RPE = good reps left" with no warning. People guess about 1 rep too few left (Halperin 2022, PMID 34542869) and about 2 in trained lifters on one test (Armes 2020, PMID 33424678), more wrongly when far from failure. Add one honest sentence. There are no RIR accuracy data in under-16s at all, so say that to parents if the lesson leans on RPE 7 for juniors.
4. **Under-16 rules are house rules, stricter than the papers.** The lesson implies they are what research says. Faigenbaum 2009 and Lloyd 2014 both accept supervised max testing and, for experienced youth, reps of 6 or fewer. Keep the rules, but label them "Amir's rules" so a parent who reads the NSCA paper isn't confused.
5. **Soften or drop two lines.** "In young players, strength comes before explosive power" is practice-based (McQuilliam 2020 says to primarily develop strength, no trial tests the order). "On a tennis court, strength for your body weight matters more than the number on the bar" could not be verified: remove or label as opinion.
6. **Course-only content correctly left out of the article:** "Club A", the 16-week fixed main lift, block names and week numbers, the RM test weeks and "The Ceiling", "the card", the Chin-Up-with-band rule, the "Coach tab" line. Chin-Up appears once as a general pulling example.
