## (c) LESSON FIXES (the TPS lesson and its English twin)
1. **"A strong aerobic base speeds recovery between points" (and the callout "an aerobic base that recovers you quickly between them")** is stated as a finding. No tennis study measures it. The accurate sources are a repeated-sprint review (energy supply incl. oxygen system limits repeated sprints) plus Baiget 2015 (fitter players play at lower relative intensity). The lesson itself already avoids a number, but "recovers you quickly" should read "helps you recover", and an honest-limit line about the mixed link with repeated sprinting would help (Gharbi 2015 moderate link, Hornlund 2024 none).
2. **The court-versus-HIIT section reads one-sided.** Both studies are on 13 to 14 year old boys. A study on 31 competitive male players found HIIT gave a bigger gain in tennis-specific endurance than repeated sprints (Fernandez-Fernandez 2012). The lesson's sentence "a good reason to build conditioning on court" is fine, but add one line that no method won everything.
3. **Yo-Yo claims are unverified.** "160 m (4 shuttles) or more is probably real" is from tests.json, no published cut-off found. "VO2max estimate can be off by a few units" has no size checked. I found no study validating Yo-Yo IR1 for tennis (reliability is shown mostly in football). Label all three as practice-based, and say the Yo-Yo is a general intermittent test.
4. **The lesson says "Yo-Yo measures the same kind of endurance tennis needs".** That is a judgement, not a finding. Soften to "has a similar pattern".
5. **RPE 7 and RPE 8 plain-words** are the author's own (the course notes already say so). Keep the label "mine".
6. **Growth-spurt halving rule** ("about half, or half on a stationary bike") has no study. Keep it as guidance from Amir and add a physio to check line (see (e)).
7. **Matches rarely sit at one intensity** is consistent with the warm-up article and is supported (Bergeron 1991, Girard 2006: the overall response resembles prolonged moderate exercise with bursts).
8. The lesson says "Court training plus repeated sprints beat court training plus HIIT ... less hard". Correct, but the abstract frames it as RPE and internal load, not "less hard". Wording kept ("enjoyed it more").

## (d) Could not verify
- Any tennis study of the exact format 6 x 8 s with 24 s rest and 75 s between games.
- That the 1 to 3 rest ratio is the "short end" for every level: Kovacs gives 1:3 to 1:5 overall (men), not by level or sex.
- A tennis-specific validity paper for Yo-Yo IR1, and the size of its VO2max estimate error.
- The 160 m "real change" cut-off.
- Padel interval training for aerobic fitness (none found), padel data on women or doubles pairs by side.
- Padel effective playing time is inconsistent between studies (30% pros, 46% regional, but one study says sub-elite lower than elite). The article gives the range.
- Kilit 2016 (PMID 28149371, one-hour simulated match, serve vs return) has no numbers in its abstract, so I did not use it.

## (e) Doctor / physio to check
- The safety callout: stop and see a doctor for chest pain or pressure, dizziness, unusual breathlessness, strange heartbeat during a maximal test. **Doctor to check** the wording.
- Growing player with pain just below the kneecap or at the back of the heel: skip running intervals until seen. **Doctor or physio to check.**
- Running the Yo-Yo (a maximal test) with an adult present: the article words it for all ages ("a coach or another adult there"). The brief says no max-test advice for juniors, so I gave no age-specific instruction. Amir to decide if he wants the Yo-Yo section in the public article at all.
