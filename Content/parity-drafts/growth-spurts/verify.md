## (c) LESSON FIXES (for the TPS lesson and its English twin, `public.tps_content` key=learn id=growth and `public.course_en` id=growth)

1. **Osgood-Schlatter wording.** Remove "the most common cause of knee pain in growing athletes and can come and go for up to about 2 years". Say instead: it is a common growth-area problem, usually settles as growth finishes, and in one older study it interfered with full training for about 7 months on average (Kujala 1985). Source for "up to 2 years" not found.
2. **"In teenagers, strength comes before explosive power"** is not a research finding. Drop it or label it as a coaching preference. Better: strength is the lowest-impact thing to keep (reasoning), with trial support for injury reduction that is not specific to growing players (Lauersen 2018).
3. **Thresholds need their source and limits.** The lesson gives 0.6 cm/month, 7 cm/year, 1.2/2 months, 1.8/3 months as if standard. 0.6 cm/month is Kemper 2015 (101 male footballers); 7.2 cm/yr is Johnson 2022 (49 male footballers) and the Hall & Erskine 2025 review. The 2 and 3 month figures are simple multiples. The "under 0.5 cm a month for 2 months" slow-down rule has no source. The lesson's honest-limits sentence says only "football research"; add "boys" (no study in girls, none in tennis or padel).
4. **Lesson says "A real growth spurt usually lasts several months."** Fine, but add that the injury-risk window may extend about a year after the peak (expert consensus, Sullivan 2024), so "full from week 3" should mean "back to plan, still watching for pain".
5. **"Depth jumps, drop jumps and max tests: none, at any age, even at 16 and 17"** is an absolute. No study supports a ban. Keep as a labelled conservative rule limited to "while the spurt lasts".
6. **"Scientists call the fastest point peak height velocity"; "bone grows first, muscles and tendons catch up"**: the second half is the common explanation, not a settled mechanism. Add "research is mixed".
7. **Home measurement**: the lesson never says a home reading can be about half a cm off. Add that so a single month is not over-read (the rule "average, or take the middle of three" partly handles it).
8. **Course-specific text removed from the article** (keep in the lesson): "the card", "the app will never tell you", "tell Amir in the Coach tab", "Snap Down", the under-16 rules (RPE 7, at least 6 reps, 60 jumps), "the lesson Pain or soreness? And red flags", the 3 x 10 pogo and 6-rep sprint examples tied to session cards (generic equivalents used).

## (d) Could not verify
- Girls' peak growth speed in cm per year (the article gives it for boys only, 7 to 12 cm from Towlson 2020).
- PHV ages 13.5 / 11.8: taken from the Saskatchewan study as quoted on the Cambridge page of PMID 19852873, whose abstract does not show them (the page also mentions 13.4). Rounded to "about". Doctor/coach may prefer a second source (Malina, Bouchard and Bar-Or textbook, not read).
- "Up to about 2 years" for Osgood-Schlatter, and "most common cause of knee pain in growing athletes": not found, removed.
- Any study of growth-related injury in tennis or padel players, and any study in girls: none found (Hall & Erskine 2025 states no included study had female players).
- Any trial showing that cutting jumps and sprints by the lesson's percentages prevents injury: none. Johnson 2023 is an uncontrolled before/after.
- Home height measurement validity and the ">1.5 cm in a month is an error" cut-off: no source.
- Lloyd 2014: only the abstract and a search-result snippet were read (the full text was blocked), so the article cites it only for "qualified adult, clean reps" and I did not attribute any growth-spurt-specific advice to it.
- Persian spelling of the two conditions as used by Iranian doctors: I used «اسگود-شلاتر» and «سیور» with the Latin name in brackets. The `_notes` ask to check; Amir or a doctor should confirm.

## (e) Doctor / physio to check
- Everything in the section "When to see a doctor or physio" and the callouts "Watch Out" under "Why knees and heels hurt". In particular: pain below the kneecap or at the heel at any level (even 1 or 2 out of 10) means see a doctor; "no jumps, sprints, braking or running conditioning until seen"; "chest pain, fainting or a blow to the head need a doctor straight away". The qualitative stance is conservative, but a sports physician should confirm the "any level of pain" threshold and the "back to full from week 3" step.
- The statement that Osgood-Schlatter and Sever's "usually settle as growth finishes" (supported by Gholve 2007 for OSD, general clinical reviews for Sever's). No treatment advice is given on purpose.
- No weight-cutting, supplement or max-testing advice appears. The low body-fat and BMI findings in Kemper 2015 were deliberately left out.
