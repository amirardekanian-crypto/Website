## (c) LESSON FIXES (TPS lesson and its English twin)

1. **Source mix-up.** The brief points to Schwellnus 2016 (IOC "How much is too much? Part 2") for return after illness. That paper is about load and illness risk, not about when to return. The IOC return-to-sport guidance is the 2022 consensus on acute respiratory illness, part 1 (Schwellnus et al, PMID 35863871, doi 10.1136/bjsports-2022-105759). It is paywalled, so I could read only its abstract. Do not cite the 2016 paper for illness return rules. Someone with access should read the 2022 return-to-sport section and check the lesson's rules against it.
2. **"A cold only above the neck and no fever: keep training as planned."** The neck-check original advice was to start at half speed and stop if you do not feel better after about 10 minutes (per the Gordon dissertation's description). The lesson is more permissive. Suggest adding "stop if you feel worse as you go". The article does.
3. **Unsourced numbers in the lesson.** "3 days below the neck, fever over 3 days: see a doctor" (borrowed from C1-v2 8.1c) and "same problem back within 4 weeks of being cleared" have no source I could find. They are practice rules. The article keeps them qualitative ("if a fever drags on", "if the same pain comes back"). Mark them as practice-based in the lesson notes, and have the doctor check them.
4. **The missed-days table is practice-based and ignores aerobic fitness.** The research says aerobic fitness falls faster than strength (claim 2). The lesson's "as written, repeat the last week" is a sensible rule for strength, and fine, but the honest limit sentence should say the numbers are practical guidance (it already does: keep it).
5. **Wording.** The lesson says "no research on coming back after a break in junior players". That stays true for what I found, but there is general research (claims 1 to 9) the lesson could point to.
6. The lesson's "an illness below the neck" and "stomach pain or diarrhoea" lines are fine and are kept as "stomach bug".

## (d) Could not verify

- Full text of Eichner 1993 (neck check). Used only through the 2014 dissertation's account of it.
- Anything newer than 2014 on whether the neck check has been tested. A web summary claimed that a 2022 Sports Medicine review said it "should be abandoned". I could not find or read that paper, so I did not use the claim.
- IOC 2022 acute respiratory illness part 1 return-to-sport section (paywalled, abstract only).
- BASES 2012 and ISEI 2011 (Walsh) statements: the "a day without fever" wording is from the dissertation's summary, not read in the originals.
- The lesson's day bands and week rules (practice-based).
- "Return to training should take as many days as you were off" appeared in the dissertation's summary of guidelines. I did not use it (lesson says every session as written, and I could not read the originals).
- Whether the Hortobagyi group results carry over to tennis or padel players.

## (e) Doctor to check

Doctor to check: the whole "After illness" and "After injury" sections before launch, in particular:
- "No fever for 24 hours without fever medicine" as the restart rule.
- The red-flag line (chest pain, feeling faint, racing or irregular heartbeat, unusual breathlessness, blow to the head).
- "If a fever drags on or you're getting worse, see a doctor" (the lesson's 3-day numbers were dropped on purpose).
- "A mild cold above the neck, no fever: you can usually train. Stop if you feel worse."
- The head-injury line ("finish the return plan the doctor gave you").
- The under-16 line.
