## (c) LESSON FIXES (TPS lesson `robustness` and its English twin `course_en`)
1. The `_notes` entry on Bohm 2015 says effects were "larger with 12+ weeks". The abstract says "although not significantly different, SMD was higher for interventions of 12 weeks or longer", so the difference was not significant. The lesson text itself does not repeat it, so only the note needs correcting.
2. The lesson's honest limit ("none of them show that training these areas prevents those problems") is right for the injury-site studies, but it leaves out the two facts a reader will meet on Google: strength training lowers injuries in sport in general (Lauersen 2014, 2018) and the one tennis trial (Pas 2020) was null. Consider adding both lines to the course lesson so the course and the blog agree.
3. The forearm section pairs eccentric wrist extension with the Achilles/elbow story. The evidence for slow loading is in people who already have tennis elbow. If the lesson implies prevention, say "ready for load, evidence is for treatment".
4. The lesson lists the Nordic Hamstring Curl and Copenhagen Plank without saying their evidence is from football (Harøy 2019, van Dyk 2019). The lesson does not claim prevention, so this is an optional add.
5. Course-only wording removed from the public article: Club A / Court B sessions, "+ robustness and mobility", "if Amir adds them", "your programme", "tap on your card", the under-16 version rule, the links to the growth-spurt, pain, jumping and braking lessons.

## (d) Could not verify
- The sport mix of the trials inside the Lauersen reviews and the NHE meta-analysis (abstract level only: "several sports"). The article says only "sport in general" and "other sports".
- That Beyer's heavy slow resistance was calf work and Peterson's lowering exercise was a wrist exercise (not in the abstracts). The article avoids "wrist" for Peterson and uses "slow heavy calf loading" for Beyer, which matches my knowledge of both trials. Worth a glance.
- Any prevention evidence for trunk exercises, the knee in tennis, calf raises against Achilles problems and Short Foot work. All are labelled in the article as coaching logic or my reasoning.
- Hamstring or groin injury rates in tennis specifically (not looked up).
- Alfredson 1998 (the classic eccentric Achilles trial) was not retrieved, so it is not cited. Silbernagel 2007 (PMID 17307888) was read but not used.

## (e) Doctor / physio to check
- The line "growing players: pain right below the kneecap or at the back of the heel needs attention" (growth-plate overuse problems).
- The red-flag line: pain that gets worse with training, wakes you at night or comes with swelling means stop training that area and see a doctor or physio.
- The statements about Achilles and tennis elbow loading describe treatment trials in already-injured people. The article gives no doses and no rehab advice.
