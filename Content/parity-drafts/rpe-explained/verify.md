## (c) LESSON FIXES (for the TPS lesson `rpe-weights` and its English twin)
1. **Accuracy callout is incomplete.** Lesson says guesses "get a bit more accurate when the set is closer to their limit" (correct) but its notes say "training status made no difference" (Halperin 2022), while Steele 2017 and Zourdos 2016 find experience helps. Keep the callout, but if the lesson ever says experience does not matter, soften it: the evidence is mixed. The "average of a few reps too few" wording overstates: the meta-analysis found about 1 rep (0.95), with a wide interval (0.17 to 1.73) and huge heterogeneity. Fix "a few too few" to "about 1 too few on average, and it varies a lot".
2. **"RPE 7 = 3 good reps left... You get stronger"**: the lesson implies RPE 7 is a physiological sweet spot. It is a scale definition. Add one clause that sets stay short of failure because failure is not required for strength gains (Grgic 2021), not because 7 is special.
3. **Lesson's RPE 6 table row ("RPE 6 with 5 or more good reps left")** is arbitrary under Zourdos (5 to 6 = 4 to 6 reps left). It is already labelled practical; leave it, but do not call it from the scale.
4. **Under-16 rule** ("RPE 7 or below, min 6 reps") is a course rule. It is fine as a caution. Do not describe it as guidance from the youth position statements: Lloyd 2014 and Faigenbaum 2009 do not set these numbers. Also the lesson's "no RM tests and no max tests" for under 16 is stricter than the consensus (which says supervised 1RM testing can be safe). It is a course decision, not a science claim: keep it labelled so.
5. Minor: the lesson's definition of RPE 10 ("next rep would be very slow or form would break") differs slightly from the paper (maximum effort, no further rep possible). Fine for practice.

## (d) Could not verify
- No study of RIR prediction accuracy in adolescents found (so the under-16 callout says so).
- The exact Helms/Zourdos %1RM x reps x RPE chart cells (only the 83% = 6RM line and the SD for 8 reps at 70% are quoted; the full table is not shown in the full text I could read).
- Refalo accuracy and Hermann accuracy are specific to bench press (and squat for Hermann); not extended to other lifts.
- The 2.5 kg step, 10% rule and weight-change table have no study behind them.
- No tennis or padel specific RPE study was used. The article makes no tennis or padel claim.

## (e) Doctor / physio to check
- The line "Pain that isn't normal muscle burn means stop the exercise and get it checked by a doctor or physio." stays qualitative on purpose. Doctor or physio to check wording if it is expanded.
- Under 16: supervision and rep or RPE limits are a course caution. A youth S&C coach or paediatric sports doctor should confirm before it is shown to parents as advice.
