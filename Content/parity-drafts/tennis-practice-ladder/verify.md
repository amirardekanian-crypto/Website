# tennis-practice-ladder series: science check for three articles (2026-11-24)

Articles (shipped 2026-11-24, English and Farsi, Farsi read and approved by Amir): `tennis-practice-ladder` (Training), `why-it-breaks-in-matches` (Training),
`train-under-pressure` (Mental). The files are `articles/<shelf>/<slug>.json` and `.fa.json`; this folder keeps only the research record for all three.

**How it was checked.** PubMed (E-utilities), OpenAlex for papers PubMed does not index, Europe PMC for open-access full text
(Czyż 2024, Bergmann 2021, Abad Robles 2020, Conde-Ripoll 2025 and Schweickle 2026 were read in part). Semantic Scholar rate-limited
and was used for title checks only. Every other paper: abstract only, and the articles quote only what the abstract says.
Dropped for lack of a readable result: Zeng et al. (2026, choking interventions, no abstract found) and Farrow & Maschette's tennis
contextual-interference study (the abstract gives no result). The newest tennis practice-structure study found is from 2019.

Verdicts: supported / supported but limited / practice-based / inference / absence claim.

## The Practice Ladder (`tennis-practice-ladder`)

| Claim | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Repetitive hitting with little game context, and unstructured game play with no goals, both leave learning short of its best | supported (review, abstract: "player learning and long-term performance are unlikely to be optimised") | Reid, Crespo, Lay et al. (2007). J Sci Med Sport. PMID 16809063. doi 10.1016/j.jsams.2006.05.011 |
| 33 juniors, 6 weeks, serve to nobody / to a returner / to a returner plus one more shot. Groups behaved differently. In matches the most match-like group chose placement on second serves, the other two speed | supported | Krause, Farrow, Pinder et al. (2019). J Sports Sci. PMID 31379253. doi 10.1080/02640414.2019.1647739 |
| Elite juniors: serve and return under 13% of practice time, each mostly practised in isolation | supported (12 elite juniors over 8 weeks against 26 players' Australian Open junior matches) | Krause, Buszard, Reid et al. (2019). J Sports Sci. PMID 31533541. doi 10.1080/02640414.2019.1665245 |
| 83 college students in tennis classes: lower-skilled students did better after blocked than alternating practice, no difference for higher-skilled | supported (abstract) | Hebert, Landin & Solmon (1996). Res Q Exerc Sport. doi 10.1080/02701367.1996.10607925 |
| Beginners who moved from blocked to mixed practice beat blocked and random schedules (golf putting, basketball passes) | supported (novices, not tennis; abstract) | Porter & Magill (2010). J Sports Sci. doi 10.1080/02640414.2010.502946 |
| 2024 review, 54 studies: mixed beats repetition in lab tasks, small and not reliable in real sport and PE settings | supported (full text: lab SMD 0.92, 95% CI 0.48 to 1.36; applied 0.23, CI -0.16 to 0.62; all studies 0.63, CI 0.33 to 0.93; "applied" = natural settings such as PE classes and games; none of the listed applied skills is a tennis stroke). Older, same pattern: Brady (2004), lab .57 vs applied .19 | Czyż, Wójcik, Solarská et al. (2024). Sci Rep. PMID 38987617. doi 10.1038/s41598-024-65753-3; Brady (2004). Percept Mot Skills. PMID 15446636 |
| Step-by-step thinking helped beginners and experts on their weaker side, and made well-learned skills worse (golfers, soccer players) | supported (putting; slalom dribbling with right and left foot) | Beilock, Carr, MacMahon & Starkes (2002). J Exp Psychol Appl. PMID 12009178. doi 10.1037/1076-898X.8.1.6 |
| How you perform during practice is often a poor guide to what you have learned | supported (review) | Soderstrom & Bjork (2015). Perspect Psychol Sci. PMID 25910388. doi 10.1177/1745691615569000 |
| 2020 review, mostly school and university studies: teaching through games improved decision making more than technical teaching, not clearly skill execution | supported but limited (7 studies on decision making, 6 on skill execution; CI 0.12 to 1.65 vs -0.45 to 2.23; large heterogeneity; GRADE low; no tennis) | Abad Robles, Collado-Mateo, Fernández-Espínola et al. (2020). Int J Environ Res Public Health. PMID 31941138. doi 10.3390/ijerph17020505 |
| 2021 review, 34 soccer papers: game-like practice with room to explore improved technique and tactics, repetition improved technique too, repetition's effect on match play largely unexplored | supported (soccer; moderate quality, large heterogeneity) | Bergmann, Gray, Wachsmuth et al. (2021). Front Psychol. PMID 34925173. doi 10.3389/fpsyg.2021.772201 |
| Table tennis: experts lost about 10%, novices 30 to 50%, when returning machine balls while doing a memory task | supported but limited (compares expert and novice players, not one stroke at two stages; table tennis) | Schaefer & Scornaienchi (2020). | 2021 review, 14 studies: practising under pressure improved performance under pressure, in novices and experienced performers; build pressure into training instead of more training | supported (g = 0.67, 95% CI 0.43 to 1.12, low heterogeneity; 10 sport and 4 law-enforcement studies; n = 394) | Low, Sandercock, Freeman et al. ## Why It Breaks in Matches (`why-it-breaks-in-matches`)

| Claim | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Skilled tennis players predict an opponent's stroke direction faster than less skilled players, partly through better visual search | supported | Williams, Ward, Knowles & Smeeton (2002). J Exp Psychol Appl. PMID 12570100. doi 10.1037/1076-898X.8.4.259 |
| With the ball in flight, players predicted serve direction better with a real return movement than with a spoken answer, and experts' edge was bigger that way | supported (Experiment 1; Experiment 2 repeated the ball-flight result) | Farrow & Abernethy (2003). Perception. PMID 14651325. doi 10.1068/p3323 |
| Teenage cricket batters, machine vs bowler at the same speed: later start and different movement against the machine; authors warn against leaning on machines | supported (12 batters, mean age 15.6; both about 28 m/s) | Pinder, Renshaw & Davids (2009). Hum Mov Sci. PMID 19339072. doi 10.1016/j.humov.2009.02.003 |
| "A basket feed is the tennis version of that machine. Nobody has tested it" | inference, written as one | none |
| People often mistake performance during practice for learning | supported (review, the metacognition section) | Soderstrom & Bjork (2015) |
| Ten-year-old girls learning a forehand: drills and games with changed equipment and rules both improved accuracy | supported (n = 24, 4 weeks; the games group also showed more varied movement solutions) | Lee, Chow, Komar, Tan & Button (2014). ## Practise Under Pressure (`train-under-pressure`)

| Claim | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Two explanations of choking (thinking about technique vs worrying); 70 expert athletes often reported worries, hardly ever the movement | supported (retrospective verbal reports and concept mapping; "often", not "most") | Oudejans, Kuijpers, Kooijman & Bakker (2011). Anxiety Stress Coping. PMID 20425657. doi 10.1080/10615806.2010.481331; Nieuwenhuys & Oudejans (2012). Psychol Res. PMID 22038472 |
| 2021 review, 14 studies, ten in sport: medium improvement under pressure, novices and experienced, open and closed tasks; build pressure into training | supported | Low et al. (2021), above |
| 17 expert basketball players, 5 weeks of free throws with or without induced anxiety: only the anxiety-trained group kept accuracy under anxiety; 17 expert dart players, climbing-wall height, same result | supported (two experiments, closed skills) | Oudejans & Pijpers (2009). Q J Exp Psychol. PMID 19123115. doi 10.1080/17470210802557702 |
| "I couldn't find this done with tennis players" | absence claim | none |
| Padel, Finland: 4,417 points, 38 matches (18 pressure-training, 20 competition), winners and errors similar | supported (observational; "pressure training" = simulated high-stakes matches; one shot-type difference in forced errors) | Conde-Ripoll, Escudero-Tena, Clemente-Suárez et al. (2025). Front Sports Act Living. PMID 40007638. doi 10.3389/fspor.2025.1505817 |
| A separate study of 10 players: more somatic anxiety and more self-confidence before competition than before training matches | supported (n = 10, men, highest category in Finland) | Conde-Ripoll, Escudero-Tena & Bustamante-Sánchez (2024). Front Psychol. PMID 38638508. doi 10.3389/fpsyg.2024.1393963 |
| Pressure can help or hurt; pressure training has to be delivered with care for wellbeing, ideally with a sport psychologist | supported (position statement) | Schweickle, Appaneal, Jackman et al. (2026). Sports Med. PMID 42271117. doi 10.1007/s40279-026-02472-x |
| The list of ways to add pressure, "add one pressure at a time", "pressure belongs on the top steps", "start small" | practice-based: **Amir to confirm or cut**. "Start small" and "calm skill first" agree with Schweickle (practise coping strategies in non-competitive settings before pressure training or competition) | none |

## TPS course lessons derived from the three articles (live 2026-11-24: Farsi in `tps_content` `learn-4`, English twins in `course_en`)

Lessons `practice-ladder` (54), `drill-to-match` (55) and `pressure-practice` (56; order 53 belongs to `recovery-between-matches`), group «تنیس و مسابقه», published to `tps_content` key `learn-4`
(the group's key) plus `course_en` twins. Drafts: `lesson.fa.json` and `lesson.en.json` in the folder of each article slug. Every study,
number and verdict above carries over unchanged ("match facts, not wording"). What differs from the articles, on purpose:

- **Register and voice.** Course register (formal شما, written verbs, «دربارهٔ»), players and parents, same shape as `match-reflection`:
  9, 7 and 7 sections, every section carries h, p, ol, list, warn, callout; paragraphs of at most 3 sentences; one-sentence summary.
- **The course's own sessions.** The ladder lesson says it is about tennis practice with the coach, not the gym sessions, which are done as written.
  The pressure lesson keeps pressure work in tennis practice, never in the course sessions.
- **Practice-based lines are labelled** "the course's own advice, not a study result" (the ladder's check-your-week questions and drop-back rule,
  the time-split sentence, the drill-to-match rule of thumb, the four upgrades, the parents' lists).
- **Junior cautions added to the pressure lesson** (the evidence is experts and adults; the ESSA statement names wellbeing risk): a warn box
  saying no study of practising under pressure with young tennis players was found; pressure is run by the coach, never a punishment, stop and go
  back a step when the player dreads the session; "family doctor or a sport psychologist" if nerves are strong or tennis stops being fun.
  Removed for juniors: "play with someone watching or film the session" and "the loser collects the balls" (replaced by "something light that
  nobody minds"). Parents' list: leave pressure practice to the coach, never tie rewards or punishments to a result, say nerves are normal.
- **Not claimed:** no number from the articles was changed and none was added, and padel is named only where the Finnish studies are.
- **Cross-references use real titles:** «روش تمرین تنیس: پنج پله تا مسابقه», «استرس مسابقه تنیس: تمرین زیر فشار» (English “The practice
  ladder”, “Practising under pressure”). Checked by script against the lessons' own titles.
- **Farsi rewritten to read as Farsi (2026-11-24).** Amir's note that day, on the recovery lessons: Farsi carried over word by word is hard to
  follow. The first drafts followed the English sentence by sentence, so each lesson's Farsi was rewritten from the ideas: short sentences, plain
  verbs, no stacked noun phrases, a result explained instead of named. Every fact and number is unchanged, checked section by section against the
  English twin by script. The first version is kept aside, not committed.
- **Covers (decided 2026-11-24, no new pictures):** each lesson reuses a picture another card already shows: `practice-ladder` gets `court-room` (a basket and a
  ladder on the clay), `drill-to-match` gets `the-split-second` (a live ball) and `pressure-practice` gets `last-ball` (a ball on the lines), by id in `ART.lesson`
  in `tennis/app/app.js`, and the app was re-stamped (`scripts/stamp_tps_app.py`). `assets/tps/` has no unused picture, so a different cover means a new picture
  (the `/image` skill, about 1 credit a candidate, 3 candidates a slot, Amir's yes first). The Library still `sessions-on-court-v1` (a basket of balls at the
  net post) is free and fits the ladder.
- **Published 2026-11-24.** The three Farsi lessons were appended to `learn-4` one at a time, each under a row lock with a `_notes` entry and `version` re-hashed,
  and the English twins went into `course_en` (sort 54, 55, 56). A text check ran inside every statement, and afterwards each stored lesson, Farsi and English,
