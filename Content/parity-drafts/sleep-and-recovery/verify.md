## (c) LESSON FIXES (the TPS lesson `sleep-recovery` and its English twin should change)
1. **Sleep extension, tennis:** "slept nearly 2 hours more" should be "about 1.7 hours more" (7.14 to 8.85 h).
2. **Sleep extension, basketball:** "stayed in bed at least 10 hours every night" overstates it. The goal was a minimum of 10 h in bed, and the measured gain was about 110 minutes of sleep.
3. **Massage:** "may reduce ... how tired you feel a little" is contested. Davis 2020 found no effect on fatigue, Dupuy 2018 did. Say the studies disagree.
4. **Bedtime example:** "in bed around 21:30" for a 6:30 wake and 9 h need. A person must be ASLEEP by 21:30, so lights out a little earlier.
5. **Athletes' sleep:** "athletes often don't get enough sleep" comes from work on ELITE athletes. Say so, or the teen reader assumes it was measured in teens.
6. **"One bad night doesn't ruin everything"** sits next to a study where one 5 h night hurt serves. Safer: "can cost some accuracy, won't wreck your season".
7. **Adults range:** NSF says 7-9 h, AASM/SRS says 7 or more. The lesson quotes only 7-9. Name the source or give both.
8. **Optional addition:** Milewski 2014 (teen athletes sleeping <8 h had 1.7 times the injury link) is in the article but not in the lesson. Add it to the lesson to keep parity, or remove it from the article.
9. **Course-only text kept OUT of the public article** (done here): Club A / Court B / Club C ordering, "your programme", "lighter weeks 4, 8, 12, 16", "+ strength and mobility", links to other lessons.
9b. **Length:** the lesson is about 1,900 words, the article limit is 1,100. Cut from the article and still in the lesson: the respiratory-infection line, the nap "doing it right" callout, relaxation and reading before a match, the long travel list (arrive early and walking stay), the "stack hard sessions" rules, and the Massage "foam roller" bullet (now one sentence). Nothing in the lesson is wrong for being longer.
10. The 6-hour caffeine rule rests on a 400 mg adult dose. If the lesson keeps "no cola or energy drinks from 6 hours before bed" for teens, label it a cautious rule.
11. Cold-water evidence is in adult men. The lesson already adds a teen adult-supervision warning. Keep it, and say there are no teen studies.

## (d) Could not verify
- AASM figures (teens 8-10 h, adults 7 or more) are not in the PubMed abstracts. Confirmed only from AASM pages through web search.
- Any padel study on sleep or recovery.
- "Daytime sleepiness or hard to wake without an alarm means you probably sleep too little": common sleep-medicine reasoning, no paper checked.
- "Walk around every hour or two on a long car or bus trip": consistent with the travel consensus (move and walk at stopovers) but the "hour or two" interval is my rule.
- Whether naps really leave night sleep alone in teens: the nap reviews are mostly adults and athletes aged 12+ pooled.
- Safe cold-water temperature and time for teenagers (none given).
- The mood result in the basketball study (POMS vigour up, fatigue down) is in the abstract, but I left the "felt fresher" line out of the short-sleep list because it is already in the extension section.
