## (c) LESSON FIXES (TPS lesson learn-5 and its English twin)
1. "Muscle ... In children and younger teens, most strength gains come this way" and the myth line "In younger teens ... from the nerves": the source (Behm 2008) says children. Change "younger teens" to "children".
2. "Supervised weight training showed no negative effect on the height or weight gain of children and teenagers": Malina 2006 covers pre- and early-pubertal youth. Change to "children and early teens" (or "before and early in puberty").
3. "Researchers put together 25 studies with more than 26,000 athletes from different sports": the abstract says 25 trials and 26,610 participants. Change "athletes from different sports" to "people" and drop "from different sports" (not in the abstract).
4. "Most of these studies were done in other sports": not verifiable from the abstract. Either say "I couldn't find a tennis or padel trial" or check the trial list.
5. Serve line "raised serve speed a little": the 2026 network meta-analysis effect (SMD 0.85) is not "a little", and the sizes are not comparable. Drop "a little". Also "accuracy usually didn't change" should read "accuracy didn't improve, and in one programme it dropped" (Martín-Miguel 2025).
6. "Players who are stronger at this usually change direction faster": "usually" rests on association studies (one basketball sample of 12) plus a narrative review. Use "tend to" and say it is a link.
7. Tendon line "heavy ... this takes a few months": the evidence is adults 18-50, and the longer-programme advantage was not significant. Say so.
8. "Consistency beats intensity" (both callouts): practice-based, no study named in the lesson. Label as coaching view, or keep the safer wording.
9. "A stronger teenager is better ready to ...": it is a narrative-review claim (Faigenbaum 2016, "will be better prepared"), not a trial finding. Say "expert reviews say".
10. "Strength gets built ... In teenagers strength training even improved sprint speed more than jump training": Behm 2017 covers children and adolescents ("youth"); "young people" is the safer word.
11. Course-only text (under-16 RPE 7 / 6-rep rules, session names, "your programme") was removed on purpose. The under-16 rules are course rules, not guideline numbers: no source supports "RPE 7, at least 6 reps" as an international standard, and the lesson itself calls them cautious.

## (d) Could not verify
- Lloyd 2014 consensus: findings and exact wording (abstract has none; full text blocked: HTTP 403/404). Used only for "endorsed by sports medicine and paediatric groups, sets out how to do it well".
- AAP 2008 policy statement (Pediatrics 121:835, PMID 18381549): abstract only says it "provides current information on risks and benefits". NOT used for any claim.
- Sport mix of the trials in Lauersen 2014 and 2018 (abstracts silent).
- Whether any tennis or padel trial tests strength training for injury prevention: my PubMed search found none (an absence in one search, not proof).
- Whether any padel strength-training trial exists: PubMed search found padel intervention studies in other designs (padel as the exercise, injury surveys, profiles) but no strength-training trial; one injury survey (Meyer 2025, PMID 40677135) only suggests strength work could help, and is not evidence for it.
- Hu 2026 is dated 28 August 2026 (very new, one network meta-analysis, Chinese-database heavy). Abstract only; I did not read the full text or the individual trials.
- Folland 2007 and Bohm 2015: abstracts only.
