2. What I collect and why

2.1 Coaching intake form

When you complete the application form at /form.html, you voluntarily submit information such as your name, email address, tennis background, training history, health-relevant information (including injuries), goals, and logistics (availability, equipment). I need this information to assess suitability for coaching and to design a safe and appropriate programme for you.

Lawful basis: performance of a contract (Article 6(1)(b) UK/EU GDPR), and, for health-relevant information you disclose about injuries or medical history, your explicit consent (Article 9(2)(a) UK/EU GDPR).

Your submission is saved directly to my own secure database, provided by Supabase (London, UK), where only I can read it — this is the durable record I work from. A copy is also delivered to my email inbox as a notification via Web3Forms, a form-relay service. Both Supabase and Web3Forms act as processors on my behalf and do not use your data for their own purposes; you can review their privacy policies on their sites.

Courses run with a partner club. Some courses are run together with a club, for example the 6-Week Padel Performance Course at The UTS in Hoylake (/uts-padel.html). The athlete form for a course like this also asks for a short health screen, an emergency contact and, for athletes under 18, a parent or guardian’s details and consent. It is stored and delivered in exactly the same way as above. Because the club runs the course with me, your answers are also shared with that club’s coaching staff, who use them only to run your course safely. The form names the club and asks for your explicit consent before you send it. The padel video you send on WhatsApp and your physical test results are used only for your movement analysis, performance profile and report, and are never published.

Lawful basis for partner courses: the same as above, plus your consent (Article 6(1)(a)) for the optional permission to share photos or clips from sessions on social media. You can withdraw that permission at any time.

If you are a coaching client using your private training app, the app records your training progress — which sessions and exercises you complete, weights logged, effort (RPE) ratings, pre-session readiness check-ins, and any notes you add. Your check-in answers also set that day’s targets in the app (on a lower day, for example, every effort target one lower); that is worked out on your phone, and the kind of day it chose, and whether you chose to train as written instead, is saved with the session. This information is saved on your device and also backed up to a secure database provided by Supabase (London, UK), so that I, as your coach, can review your progress and support your training. Only I can access it; Supabase acts as a processor on my behalf. This data is kept for as long as you are an active coaching client and removed on request.

2.2 Programme app data

Read the details

If I build a programme for you, you open it at amirardekani.com/program.html and sign in with the username and password I give you. The programme itself is kept in the same private database as your progress (below), readable only by you and me. The app also uses your browser’s local storage on your device to remember your progress (completed sessions, weight logs, RPE scores, session notes, readiness answers, and interface preferences), so that it works without a signal. It also keeps a copy of the last version of your programme it loaded, so that the programme opens without one too (in a basement gym, for example). Only one programme is ever kept this way: yours is replaced each time the app loads it again, and it is deleted when you sign out, if someone else signs in on the same device, or if my server stops accepting your sign-in. Signing out (your initials, top right, then Sign out) first sends anything still waiting, and your progress stays on the phone for your next sign-in. The habit tracker (section 2.3) reads your name from the same copy, so it opens without signal too. Clearing your browser data deletes it.

This progress is also backed up to my database so that I can coach you properly: I need to see whether you finished a session, what you lifted, and what you told me about it. The backup is hosted on Supabase (London, UK). Your record is tied to your own sign-in (a username and password), and nobody but you and me can read it — other athletes cannot. The app no longer has a chat: you message me on WhatsApp, which keeps those conversations under its own privacy policy. Messages you sent through the app’s old chat (removed on 26 September 2026) stay in the same database until you ask me to delete them. If you clear your browser data, the local copy is deleted but the backup remains until you ask me to delete it (see section 6).

When you finish a session (you rate it and tap Done), the app also emails me a summary of it: the day, the exercises and what you logged, how long it took, your readiness answers, your rating and your note. It goes through Web3Forms, the same form-relay service as the application form. If you have no signal, the summary waits on your phone and goes by itself once you do; it is kept only on that phone, never copied to another device, and deleted from it once it has gone.

The app also has a Library of ready-made workouts — warm-ups, mobility flows, recovery sessions and full sessions — that are not part of your own programme. If you open one and press Mark as done, I store a single line saying which workout it was and the date you did it. Nothing else: no weights, no notes, no exercise-by-exercise record. It is used so that your habit tracker can tick the matching habit for that day, and so that I can see what you have been doing between our sessions. Pressing the button is the only thing that reaches me — opening a workout and reading it does not. When the app suggests one of these workouts (after a check-in that says you are sore, or once every session of your week is done), it notes on your phone only when you last opened that workout, so the suggestion goes away once you have looked at it; that note is never sent anywhere.

The app also keeps your Personal Records (called The Ceiling until 26 September 2026) — an estimate of the most you could lift once, for each exercise. Normally it is not a test you take: when you finish a session, the app works one out from the sets you logged, using the weight you used, the reps, and the effort (RPE) you gave it, and if it beats your best on that lift it adds it to your records by itself. You can also enter one yourself from the Personal Records screen, choosing a lift from your current programme and, if you like, dating it to the day you actually lifted it. Some lifts carry a note from me asking you to retest them every few weeks; that is a suggestion on your screen, not something the app does on its own. Either way, what is kept is the lift’s name, the estimated number, the set it came from, the date, whether it came from a test, and whether the app added it by itself — in the same private record as the rest of your programme progress, readable only by you and me. It earns no points anywhere, and the crew never sees a weight from it. If you are in the crew, they see the name of the lift when you set a new best and how many personal bests you have, as long as your switch for personal bests is on (section 2.12). You can delete any entry from its row in that lift’s history, and the deletion follows to every device you use: the app will not add it back.

That screen also shows your strength relative to your body weight, because for a racket-sport player that ratio matters more than the raw kilos. To do that it uses your most recent body-weight reading (section 2.3) — the same private record, your own data, shown only back to you on your own device. If you have never logged a weight, the app asks you to add one rather than showing anything.

Share cards (Highlight Reel). The app can turn one of your wins (a new best, a win this cycle, a retest you beat, a finished cycle or the whole plan) into a picture for Instagram. The picture is drawn on your phone, from data already on it. Nothing leaves your phone until you tap Share or Save image, and then it goes only where you send it: your phone’s own share sheet, or your downloads. I never post anything for you. A card shows your first name (you can switch it off), the lift and its numbers (you can hide the weights, so only the change shows), the date, and my name, photo and signature. It never shows your body weight, your check-in answers or anything about pain, and a card about the crew (one of the month’s honours, or your result in The Open) never names another athlete: it shows your own place and how many took part. The size, language and two switches you choose are remembered on that phone only.

Your own photo on a card. You can put a picture from your phone behind any card, and move or zoom it until it sits where you want. The picture is read by the app on your phone, made smaller on your phone and drawn on your phone. It is never uploaded: it is not sent to me, it is not stored by the app, and it is forgotten as soon as you close the card. Only the finished picture goes anywhere, and only when you tap Share or Save image.

If you signed up through a partner tennis academy rather than directly with me, a summary of your training and recovery goes to your coach at that academy. That is part of the arrangement between me and the academy, and it is stated on the sign-up form before you join. It covers what you did and when — never your messages to me, your health answers, your body weight or your leaderboard activity.

Lawful basis: contract (Article 6(1)(b)) — delivering the coaching you have paid for.

2.3 Habit tracker and leaderboard

Read the details

The habit tracker at amirardekani.com/habits.html (the same sign-in) works the same way: what you tick off each day is stored on your device first and backed up to the same private record. Your points and levels are calculated from that history rather than stored as a record of their own. Since October 2026 your level is a career level: it counts what you log in the tracker and the sessions you finish in the programme app, and it no longer resets each season. Only the points on the board start again. To work them out, the server keeps a list of the things you did that count: a session finished, a day’s habits, a week of sessions held, a personal best. So that the board loads quickly, it also keeps a copy of your current numbers. Both are made again from your history whenever it changes, and both are erased with the rest of your data. Since 18 October 2026 the server also keeps a record of what you have earned: the achievements and marks you have reached (a mark is a number of weeks held, sessions, personal bests or comebacks), the day you reached each, and which of the things in that list earned it. Since 19 October 2026 there are no titles and no other badges: those records were deleted, and the points they had given you were kept as plain points on the same days. It also notes whether the app has shown you each moment yet, so that you see each one once. A reward stays yours after the scores reset each season, so this record is kept and is never taken back. You can correct or fill in the last few days of your own log inside the app; older days are closed to editing.

What I note about your progress (since 19 October 2026). My dashboard shows me the bigger moments you reach (a run of weeks held, a number of sessions, personal bests, comebacks, and the achievements), so that I can send you a message on WhatsApp. When I have seen one, or sent you that message, the server keeps a note of it against your athlete ID, so that the app can later tell you I saw it. At the start of each month the server works out that month’s honours (most plan sessions, most improved on a main lift at the same effort, every plan session done, and my own pick) from the records above, and keeps the names of the athletes who won them, with the number behind each. I can also write you one goal for the month and tick it when you have done it, and write you one short line of my own; both are kept against your athlete ID, and the app will show them to you. The crew sees part of this, only if you are in it: the moments, achievements and honours of the month that section 2.12 describes, on your page and in the Bell, following your switches in What the crew sees of you. The rest is for you and me: comebacks, the notes of what I have seen, your goal and my line for you. All of it is erased with the rest of your data, and it gives no points.

The crew sees your moments, achievements and honours of the month only if you are in it, and only the kinds you leave switched on (section 2.12). No one else does. Until 19 October 2026 the tracker could show a title beside your name on the leaderboard (the board is described below). It no longer shows a title, a rank name or a badge to anyone but you. If you delete your data, this record and the list behind it are deleted with the rest (see section 6) and leave my backups within about three months, as everything else does.

Body weight is recorded in the programme app, not the habit tracker (it moved there in September 2026; readings taken before the move came with it and nothing was lost). It is a standard part of the programme app, and nothing is recorded until you enter a reading. Each reading you enter is kept as a date and a number in kilograms, in the same private record as the rest of your training history, and is used for one purpose: so that you and I can see the trend while I coach you. It is never shown to other athletes — not on the leaderboard, not in Roll Call, not in any form — and it earns no points, so it cannot appear indirectly through your score either. You can delete any single reading from its row in the history, or every reading you have ever logged in one action from the same screen. You can still ask me to delete them instead, or as part of deleting the rest of your data (see section 6).

Lawful basis: legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f) UK/EU GDPR) — letting you and me see the trend behind a number you have chosen to log, as part of the coaching relationship, weighed against your right to object to this specific processing at any time: nothing is recorded unless you enter a reading yourself, and every reading can be deleted in one action from the same screen, rather than by a form or a request to me.

The tracker includes a leaderboard, and everyone training with me is on it. You are added automatically once you have finished your first workout in the programme app, rather than being asked to join. The app tells you this before it happens — its tour and the Crew tab show the name you will appear under, let you change it, and let you keep yourself off the board in advance with one tap — and again afterwards: a banner on the day screen says you are on the board, shows the name other athletes see, and takes you straight to the setting — not only in this notice. You can leave at any time from inside the app (Settings → Crew board), and leaving removes your entry immediately and for good: you are not added back. While you are on it, other athletes can see the display name shown on that banner — by default your first name and the initial of your surname, for example “Alex R.”, which you can change to anything you like, including something that is not your name — and your points. Since 19 October 2026 the board shows no rank name and no title beside a name. Your points on the board also count the sessions you finish in the programme app, as a number only: other athletes never see what you trained. Your habits, your logs, your programme, your real name and your athlete ID are never shared. Only signed-in athletes can view the board; it is not public, and it is not indexed or shared outside it. One board is shared by everyone using the tracker — both my coaching clients and people using the free tracker without coaching — so the athletes who can see your entry are not only my clients. You can rename yourself or leave at any time from inside the app, and leaving removes your entry immediately.

Being on the board also lets you use Roll Call: one short sentence a day that other athletes on the board can read. This is entirely voluntary — you never have to write one, and the tracker works exactly the same if you do not. If you do write one, three things are visible to the other athletes on the board for seven days: your display name, the sentence you wrote, and how much of that day’s habits you had completed, as a single percentage. Habits are not all worth the same — a training session counts for more than a supplement — so that one number is weighted, and a reader who knows the weights could make a reasonable guess at which habits were behind it. It is still one number: the app never sends the list. Please do not put anything sensitive in it — in particular nothing about your health, injuries, medication or anything else you would not want another client to read. You can change or delete your sentence at any time before midnight, after which it stays as written. Leaving the leaderboard immediately hides every sentence you have written, and you can ask me to delete them outright (see section 6). I can also remove any line from the wall myself.

Lawful basis: legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f) UK/EU GDPR) for the leaderboard entry itself — a shared board is part of how this coaching programme works, and the information shown is limited to a changeable display name and a score, weighed against your right to object at any time by leaving, which is a single tap inside the app rather than a request to me. It is not described as consent, because you are added without being asked and consent has to be a real choice made in advance. Roll Call remains consent (Article 6(1)(a)) — nothing is shown unless you write it, and you can delete it.

2.4 Jump test

The jump test at amirardekani.com/jump.html is different from everything else on this page: nothing you put into it ever leaves your device. There is no account, no login and no backend.

Your video is never uploaded. When you choose a clip, your browser reads the file directly from your phone and analyses it there. It is not sent to me, it is not sent to Supabase, and it is not sent to any third party. Close the page and the video is gone from the app entirely.

Three things are saved in your browser’s local storage, on your device only, under the keys aa_jump_cfg, aa_jump_log and aa_jump_meta: the details you enter about yourself (a name if you give one, standing height, and optionally body mass, sex, age and footwear), the conditions of each test, and your saved results. I cannot see any of it. There is a Clear all jump data on this device button in the app that deletes the lot, and clearing your browser data does the same.

The page loads fonts from Google Fonts, which is noted in section 2.8. Nothing else on the page makes a network request.

Lawful basis: not applicable — no personal data reaches me, so there is no processing by me to have a basis for. The storage on your device is strictly necessary for the tool you asked to use.

2.6 Analytics

I use Plausible Analytics to understand aggregate, anonymous visit patterns (page views, referrer, country, device type). Plausible is a privacy-first, cookie-less analytics service hosted in the EU. It does not set cookies, does not track users across sites, and does not collect personally identifiable information. No individual user can be identified from the data I receive.

Lawful basis: legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f)) — measuring aggregate traffic to improve the site. Because no identifiers are stored or returned to your device, this processing falls outside the scope of PECR / ePrivacy cookie-consent requirements.

2.7 Embedded video (YouTube)

Exercise demonstration videos are embedded from YouTube. Nothing is requested from YouTube until you tap a video’s play button: the player is only loaded at that moment. When you do, Google (YouTube’s operator) may set cookies and collect data according to Google’s privacy policy. I have no control over that processing. If you prefer not to share data with Google, do not press play on embedded videos.

2.8 Hosting, server logs, fonts and code libraries

This site is hosted on GitHub Pages. GitHub may retain temporary request logs (including IP address and user-agent) for security, abuse prevention, and service operation. See GitHub’s privacy statement. I do not receive or access these logs.

Fonts. The home page, the apply and free-tracker forms, the privacy and terms pages, the Farsi pages and the Etminan pages serve their typefaces from this site, so those pages make no font request to Google. Some other pages (the articles, the training and habit apps and the UTS page) still load their typefaces from Google Fonts. To draw them, your browser asks Google’s servers for the font files, which tells Google your IP address and the address of the page. Google explains what it does with that in its Google Fonts privacy FAQ. I do not receive that data.

Code libraries. The training and habit apps, the pages you sign in to, load the code library that connects them to my database (Supabase’s) from jsDelivr, a free code delivery network. To fetch it, your browser asks jsDelivr’s servers for that file, which tells jsDelivr your IP address and the address of the page. jsDelivr explains what it does with that in its privacy policy. I do not receive that data.

I use an AI assistant, Claude, made by Anthropic, to help me write your programme and the notes in it. I read, change and approve every programme myself before you see it, and the assistant does not decide whether I coach you.

Anthropic acts as a processor on my behalf and handles this data under its own privacy policy and terms; it may process it outside the UK and the EEA (section 5). If you have a question about this, or want your data deleted, message me (section 6).

Lawful basis: the same as section 2.1: performance of a contract and, for health-relevant information, your explicit consent.

2.10 Course and testing-app accounts

If you buy the Tennis Performance System course or the tennis testing app, I create a sign-in for you: a username and a password. I keep the username, the date it was made, whether it is active, and a short private note of my own about who bought it, which only I can read. The sign-in service stores your password as a one-way hash. The first password I generate for you is also held in my database until you sign in with it, and then it is deleted.

The course is downloaded to your device when you sign in. It does not send me your progress, your answers or what you have read.

The testing app stores what you enter in it in my database, hosted on Supabase (London, UK): the players you add (their name, birth year and month, sex, handedness and squad), your notes about them and their test results. It is kept so that you can use the app from any device. I do not read it or use it for anything else. It is included in my weekly backup, which I can technically open, and I do not. Removing a player in the app only hides them; when you ask me to delete your account, the hidden ones go too, and if you want one player’s details gone sooner, ask me.

Who decides about the players. You choose to put that information in, and it should only be about people you are allowed to record. You decide why you record it and how it is used, so for the players you enter you are the one responsible for it and I only store it for you. If your players include children, you are responsible for having the right to record their details. If you are a player, or a parent, and a coach or academy entered your details, ask them first: they decide, and I will help them answer you.

Ask me and I delete your account and everything stored under it, in one step. There is no notice period.

Lawful basis for your account: performance of a contract (Article 6(1)(b)): you bought the product and it cannot work without the account. For the players you enter, the lawful basis for recording them is yours to have.

2.11 The website chat

The home pages and the Farsi product pages have a chat button. The answers come from an AI assistant, Claude, made by Anthropic, working only from a page of facts about my coaching that I wrote. It is not me, and it says so. To answer, what you type is sent to Anthropic, which acts as a processor on my behalf (section 5).

I keep each chat in my database on Supabase (London, UK), readable only by me, so I can see what people ask and fix answers that were wrong: the messages, the time, the page you were on and whether it sent you to WhatsApp or the application form. It does not ask for your name, email or number, and it does not store your IP address. To stop one person flooding it, it keeps a scrambled code made from your IP address and the date, which changes every day and cannot be turned back into the address. Please do not type health details or anything private: for anything about you, message me on WhatsApp instead. Your browser keeps this tab’s copy of the chat until you close the tab (session storage).

Every chat is deleted 90 days after its last message. Because a chat carries no name, I cannot look yours up by who you are; tell me roughly when you chatted and what you asked, and I will delete it sooner.

Lawful basis: legitimate interests (Article 6(1)(f)): answering visitors’ questions about my coaching and improving those answers, weighed against your interests by collecting no name or contact, storing no IP address and deleting everything after 90 days. You choose whether to use it.

2.12 Crew, the Bell and your page (the programme app)

The programme app has a Crew tab, shared by the athletes signed in to it. Nothing of yours appears there unless you say yes. The first time you open Crew the app asks Join the crew?, and you can change your answer at any time (your initials → Crew and the Bell). If you say no, the crew sees nothing of yours.

If you say yes, the other athletes signed in to the programme app can see, under your board name (section 2.3), for 7 days:

  • Your moments, as one line a day: a new level, a training week held and how many in a row, a mark you reached (for example 8 weeks held or 50 sessions), an achievement, a personal best (the name of the lift only, never the weight), a cycle finished, an honour of the month, and that you joined the crew. You can switch any of these off one by one. Never shown: weights, reps, RPE, your check-in answers, pain or injuries, body weight, your habits, a week you missed or a single comeback.
  • What you post: a message of up to 280 characters and, if you add one, a photo. Your phone makes the photo smaller before it is sent (1080 pixels on its longest side, about 200 KB), and saving it again removes its hidden data, including where it was taken. At most 3 photos a day.
  • Likes and emoji: when you like a post or a line, or send it one of the four emoji, the crew sees your board name with it. One per person: sending another swaps it. Liking the line that tells an achievement also leaves your name on that achievement, and that name stays (see Names on achievements below).
  • The Open (since 19 October 2026): one challenge I set for the whole crew. You do it and send me the clip on WhatsApp; nothing is stored from the clip, and the app never takes a result by itself. When I confirm your result, the crew sees your board name and the number on the board. A result is a record, not a post: it is not deleted after 7 days. It stays while the challenge is up and after I close it, and it goes when your data is erased. It earns no points, and a result I record for someone who has not joined the crew is never shown to anyone but me.

Every post, photo, like and emoji is deleted from my database 7 days after it was posted, and these are left out of my weekly backups on purpose, so that promise holds. The one thing that outlives a like is the name it leaves on an achievement (see Names on achievements below). You can delete your own post at any time, and I can remove any post. Leaving later deletes your posts, photos and likes straight away. 2.13 Reminders on your phone

Both apps can send a reminder to your phone: one at the end of the day if you have not logged your habits, and one if you have not trained for a couple of days. Nothing is sent until you turn them on. Your phone asks you first, and no app of any kind can answer that question for you.

When you say yes, your browser gives me an address for that phone — a long code, made by Google (on an Android) or Apple (on an iPhone), that lets me send a notification to that one app on that one phone. I store it with the hour you chose, how many quiet days you want before the training one, which kinds you have left on, your phone’s offset from UTC (so a reminder arrives in your evening, not mine) and a short line naming the phone and browser, so I can tell which one stopped working. That address cannot be used to read anything on your phone, to find where you are, or to identify you to anyone else.

To send one, that address and the words of the reminder go to Google or Apple, who carry it to your phone. The words are encrypted: they can carry it but cannot read it, and only your phone can open it. A reminder never contains a weight, a number of reps, an RPE, a check-in answer, pain or injury, your body weight or which habits you missed — a lock screen is read by whoever picks the phone up. It says only that your day is still open, or that it has been a couple of days.

Nothing is ever sent before 8am or after 10pm in your own time, and never more than two reminders in a day (the habits one and the training one). Notifications from the crew have their own rule (section 2.12). You can turn any kind off, change the hour, or turn them all off, in the app (AA Proof: Settings → Reminders; the programme app: your initials → Reminders). Turning them off deletes that phone’s address from my database straight away. You can also block notifications for this site in your browser, which stops them whatever the app says. If you uninstall the app or withdraw the permission, the address stops working and I delete it the next time I try to use it.

Lawful basis: consent (Article 6(1)(a) UK/EU GDPR). You give it by turning reminders on, your phone asks you separately, and you can withdraw it at any time in the app or in your browser settings, with no effect on anything else.
