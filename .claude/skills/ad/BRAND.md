**This file is the interview, not my inference.** Every line below came from Amir answering a
direct question on 2026-09-21, or from a source named beside it. Where I am guessing, it says
**[assumed]**. Where nothing has been said yet, it says **[GAP]** — fill it, do not invent it.

Read this before writing a single word of an ad. `STRATEGY.md` turns it into angles; `SKILL.md`

| | |

⚠️ **Organic changes the physics of an ad.** Money cannot buy a stranger's attention here, so an ad
has to **travel** — be saved, or sent to a friend — even though the number being counted is WhatsApp
messages. An ad that converts but nobody shares reaches only the people already following him. Every
ad needs a reason to be kept. This is the single constraint most likely to be forgotten.

## 2. Who is actually watching

Amir's own answer, not the target he wishes for. Roughly **5,000–20,000 followers.**

| Who | What they mean for an ad |
|---|---|
| **Tennis & padel players in Iran** | The buyer. Ads are mostly conversion, not discovery. |
| **Other coaches and trainers** | They will not buy a player's course, but they **share**, and they send players. ⚠️ This is why attacking coaches is forbidden in §5 — it burns his own distribution. |
| **General fitness / gym people** | Left over from the pre-2026-09 Farsi positioning. Big, wrong-fit, drags reach without buying. |

⚠️ **Parents are NOT in the audience.** Amir did not pick them. The course has a teen version
(13–15), a growth-spurt lesson and a parents' guide, so a parent-facing ad is a real product fit —
but it can only reach them **second-hand, when a player sends it to them**. Brief it as a
share-driven ad, never as cold reach. (This corrected my first ad board, which had it as the
flagship.)

### What they say is wrong — in the order he hears it

**In their words, as Amir reported them 2026-09-21** — quote these, do not improve them:

1. **"In the third set my energy is low and my legs are tired."**
   ⚠️ **Legs, not lungs.** The fix they imagine is running; the fix is strength endurance and
   braking. That gap between what they'd do and what works is an ad on its own.
2. **"I'm not quick to the balls — I want to be faster."**
   ⚠️ Stated as a **desire**, not a complaint. It can open an ad either way.
3. **"Some kind of injury — knee pain from deceleration."**
   ⚠️ **Deceleration**, not "my knee hurts". Braking is trainable, and untrained braking is where
   the load goes. This turns P3 from a referral-only topic into one with a real training answer
   (`speed-braking`, `robustness`) — while the doctor referral stays in frame (§5.3).
4. **"Going to the gym will make me slower or bulky."** — see the fourth objection below.

⚠️ He did **not** pick "I go to the gym but don't know what to do for tennis." Do not build an ad
on the transfer-from-the-gym problem and call it a customer problem; it is an argument Amir finds
true, not a sentence anyone sends him.

**Also reported, 2026-09-21:** plateau («هر چی تمرین می‌کنم بهتر نمی‌شم»), losing to players with
worse technique, and not knowing how much weight to lift. He did **not** report lack of time.

**What they want** (his ticking): win the matches they are currently losing · keep playing for years
without breaking down · move like an athlete rather than a club player. He did **not** pick status,
ranking or team selection.

### Why they do not buy — all three are the same objection

1. **They do not trust an online course from anyone.**
2. **They think they can find it free on YouTube.**
3. **They want a real coach watching them.**

### And a fourth, which is the one they say out loud first

**Most people open with «قیمت؟» before any value has been established** (Amir, 2026-09-21).
**He thinks $17 is very cheap. They hear it as expensive.** That is not a price problem, it is a
**unit problem** — they are comparing $17 against nothing.

The fix is an anchor, and the honest one is his own coaching: the personal programme is
**معادلِ ۲۵ دلار در ماه**, so the same 16 weeks costs about **$100**. The course covers those 16
weeks for **$17**, at their own speed, with no expiry. Roughly six times less, for the same period,
and every number is checkable on his own pages.

⚠️ **Amir said "3 times this price for a month" on 2026-09-21; his live Farsi page says $25/month,
which is 1.5×.** The per-period comparison above is both true and stronger, so it is what the
templates use. **If he has raised the Iran coaching price, `index-fa.html`, `Content/PRODUCT.md` and
`WHATSAPP.md` all need to move together** — a sales line that contradicts the public page is worse
than no line.

### The fourth objection — a belief, not a format

**«باشگاه منو کند و حجیم می‌کنه»** — *"the gym will make me slow or bulky."* (Amir, 2026-09-21.)

⚠️ **This is the only objection that stops them before they even consider a course**, and it is the
one Amir's own argument explains: they are afraid of the gym because **the gym they have seen is
bodybuilding** — which is his complaint (1), from the other side. Their fear and his annoyance are
the same fact.

So the answer is never "no it won't". It is: **you are right to be afraid of that kind of training —
it is the wrong tool for tennis, and it is not what this is.** Heavy-but-low-volume strength,
plyometrics, braking and change of direction do not build the body they are picturing. Own the fear,
then redirect it at the method. Angle A12.

⚠️ **The first three objections attack the format, not the price.** Self-guided, online, pre-made,
nobody watching. $17 is not the real obstacle even when price is the word they use. So an ad that only *describes the course well* does nothing — it has to
answer *why him*, *why not free*, and *why nobody is watching me*. An ad that answers none of the
three is not an ad, it is a brochure.

## 3. The position

> **The coach who knows more than your coach.**

What Amir wants a viewer thinking when the ad ends, in his words: **"this guy knows more than my
coach."** Authority. Not relatability, not inspiration, not "I could do this."

**His argument — what other coaches get wrong.** These are his, picked from a list, and they are
the engine of most angles:

1. **They train players like bodybuilders.** Splits and machines for a sport that brakes, rotates
   and repeats.
2. **They only do conditioning — running laps.** Fitness confused with running. Nothing transfers.
3. **Nobody measures anything.** No baseline, no retest, no way to know if four months worked.

⚠️ **(3) is the spine of the whole brand.** It is the one belief that answers his argument, the
"free on YouTube" objection *and* the position at once: free content gives exercises, it cannot give
you a baseline and a retest. A man with a stopwatch looks like someone who knows more than your
coach. Lead with measurement when there is no reason to lead with something else — see
`STRATEGY.md` §A1.

### What the product is — his words (2026-09-21)

The first statement of the value proposition from Amir rather than from the page:

- **The cheaper way to get his method** than the personal coaching he sells.
- **Structured progression** — it builds on itself, rather than being a pile of workouts.
- **Education, not just prescription** — how to perform each movement, plus the lessons.
- **Everything a tennis player needs, in one place.**
- **Home, gym and on court** — all three.
- **Every physical quality a tennis player needs, based on science.**

⚠️ **Never phrase it as the "cheap version".** Same method, self-guided, at a price anyone can do.
What differs is who does the thinking week to week, not the quality of the training.

**The existing mantra still holds:** «هیچی بی‌دلیل نیست» — nothing without a reason. It is already
the coaching app's spine (`Content/PRODUCT.md`) and it is the same idea as (3) pointed inward.

## 4. Him, on camera

**He will film himself — face and voice.** (2026-09-21. Every reel before this was animation with
no human in it.) This is the biggest asset the brand was not using: against "I don't trust online
courses" and "I want a coach watching me", nothing generated competes with the actual coach saying
the thing.

- **Register: teacherly. He explains things.** Calm, structured, a coach at a whiteboard — not hype,
  not buddy-buddy. It is also the register that matches the position exactly.
- **He can film often. Editing is what stops him,** and he edits **in Instagram's own editor**,
  which cannot do what these ads need.
- ⚠️ **Therefore the pipeline is: Claude scripts → Amir films one take on his phone → he sends the
  clip → Claude assembles → he posts.** Never hand him parts to assemble. Generated footage exists
  to make that final cut, not to become his homework. `SKILL.md` stage 16 (the editing plan) is the assembly.
- **[GAP] His story — why he does this.** Asked 2026-09-21, not yet answered. Now that he is the
  face of it, this is the missing piece of the trust layer, and the whole of angle A7.

### Proof he can honestly use

- **Coaching clients' real results — with their permission.** 48 programmes and 435 logged sessions
  exist behind them. ⚠️ Permission is per athlete and per claim; never assume it.
- **His credentials.** Two MSc's (S&C, Applied Exercise Physiology), 7+ years, **1000+ tennis and
  padel players**, 15 years competitive tennis. Needs nobody's permission.
- **The app itself, on screen.** Showing the real thing working is proof of substance — what reel 7
  already does.
- **Not the course's results.** There are none yet (§1).

## 5. Advertising principles — the hard rules

The first four are Amir's own decisions on 2026-09-21. He was offered the harder version of each
and **chose the honest one every time**; that is the ruling, not a compromise I imposed.

1. **Attack the method, never the person.** «دویدن دور زمین، بدنسازی تنیس نیست» — yes. «مربیت داره
   اشتباه تمرینت می‌ده» — no. Same fight, no named enemy. A coach can share the first without losing
   face. Never name, imply or caricature another coach or academy.
2. **Promise the retest, never the result.** «۴ هفته دیگه دوباره تست می‌دی، عددت رو خودت می‌بینی» —
   yes. «تو ۶ هفته سریع‌تر می‌شی» — no. The retest is certain and true; a timeline on an outcome is
   neither, and he would have to defend it with two MSc's behind him.
3. **Teach the difference, never scare.** Soreness vs pain, drawn the way the `pain-red-flags`
   lesson draws it — yes. «این درد زانو الان درست نشه، بعداً سخت‌تره» — no. The course's own callout
   is «رشد سریع سالم است و علامت خطر نیست»; an ad built on fear would be arguing with the product it
   is selling.
4. **No hype editing.** No punch-zooms, no shouting, no countdown timers, no cheap lead-gen energy.
   Premium, clinical-but-warm, breathing room — the content has to justify a premium price
   (`Content/DESIGN-ATLAS.md`).
5. **No invented numbers, results or names.** The standing accuracy gate. He fact-checks physiology.
   Simple round figures, no on-screen maths.
6. **Generated footage is atmosphere, never evidence.** It shows an invented athlete: never "my
   client", never a before/after, never a result laid over it. Carried over from
   `.claude/skills/video/SKILL.md`, and it binds here too.
7. **One ask per ad.** Never stack WhatsApp *and* the demo *and* a follow. See `STRATEGY.md` §CTA.

**Look rules** (unchanged, from `Content/DESIGN-ATLAS.md` and `.claude/skills/image/SKILL.md`):
clay `#C7552F` is the only accent, clay-2 `#E06B43` on dark · green `#0E4A36` · paper `#FAF7F2` ·
**no yellow, no gold, ever** · Vazirmatn for Farsi, no uppercase, no letter-spacing, Persian
numerals, RTL · real over synthetic · never depict exercise form · 1080×1920, and everything that
must be read sits between y≈250 and y≈1600 because Instagram covers the rest.

## 7. The field

Who Amir says he is standing next to — he did **not** say "nobody":

- **Tennis coaches who post some fitness content.** Technique coaches straying into fitness. His two
  MSc's are the wedge.
- **Big general fitness accounts.** Loud, high production, nothing tennis-specific. Easy to look
  different from, hard to out-reach.
- **Foreign English-language accounts players already follow.** The real competition for attention:
  better produced, not in Farsi, and not reachable for coaching.

## 8. The funnel after the ad

Ad → bio link → `/tennis/` (or the demo at `/tennis/app/?demo=1`) → WhatsApp → card-to-card → login
sent over WhatsApp.

⚠️ **Amir answers every WhatsApp message personally, with no template.** Good for trust, and it is
the leakiest point in the whole system: a slow reply loses a sale that an ad already paid for in
reach. **The ad's last line and his first reply should carry the same promise, word for word.**
Writing him that first-reply template is part of the CTA layer, not an afterthought — `STRATEGY.md`
§CTA.

**Tracked already:** Plausible goals `Demo opened`, `Demo failed`, `Demo locked`, `Demo buy` fire on
`/tennis/app/?demo=1`. Nothing tracks WhatsApp — that number is Amir counting.
