# /cut research, 2026-10-01: what can make the reels better

Amir asked (2026-10-01): *"do a deep research about motion graphics and edits to see if there are any skills regarding these that can help us with
edits. for example skills on github we can learn from."*

Five read-only research agents covered five areas, and I then checked the key claims on this PC and on the primary pages. Their full reports are in
`research-2026-10-01/` (they hold the file:line pointers). **Nothing was installed, downloaded, uploaded or paid for.** No page the agents read tried
to redirect them.

Marks used below: **[V]** I checked it myself today on this PC or on the primary page. **[A]** an agent read it in the source and I did not re-check.
**[U]** unverified. Verdicts: **ADOPT** take it (as is or with small changes), **LEARN** take the idea or the numbers, not the code, **SKIP** does not fit
(cloud or credits, captions, wrong stack, wrong licence, wrong language).

## 1. Bottom line

1. **No third-party skill is worth installing as it is.** About 35 repos and the 21 Hyperframes skills were read. None handles Farsi or right-to-left
   type, and none makes his kind of reel (his own footage, type behind him, domain graphics). Most are English product films. What they have that we lack is
   **process and numbers**: QA loops, per-reel caps, a variety audit, cutting rules. Copy those into `/cut`. Installing someone else's skill adds licence
   and security risk and pulls in glow, mesh, yellow and captions.
2. **The best help is already on this PC.** Hyperframes ships 21 skills and we use 7. The two unused ones that matter are `hyperframes-cli` (the `check`
   gate) and `hyperframes-keyframes` (punch-ins and speed ramps inside one render).
3. **Five things worth doing, in order:** (1) align every sound's hit with its picture event [V, section 2.1]; (2) guard his caption band with
   `hyperframes check` [V, 2.2]; (3) a QA gate: variety audit, per-reel caps, pop scan, phone-size sheet; (4) better cutting logic in `plan_to_clips.py`
   (floor-calibrated threshold, retake flags, seam fades); (5) a Farsi word-timing trial (needs downloads, section 7). Plus one real-phone test of the safe zone [2.3].
   **Status (the same night, after his "yes, apply the free fixes"): (1) to (4) are applied and proven, and the Farsi word timing (5) turned out to need no big download: flash attention
   off plus a clamp fixed it (section 14). The VAD file was fetched and tested and is not used. The phone test card is made and waits for his phone.**
4. **What we already do better than every repo:** whole-word Farsi RTL tied to his real speech, domain graphics (force-time curve, muscle map, 3D court),
   type behind him with a studio swap, and restraint (one accent, free top band, no captions, few elements).

## 2. What I verified myself [V]

### 2.1 Our sounds land late
I measured all 34 kit sounds (5 ms windows, ffmpeg decode, script: `tools/sfx_lead.py`, same numbers as below).

| Group | Files | When the sound really starts and hits |
|---|---|---|
| 23 mp3 (the menu's sounds) | ball-bounce, ball-hit, boom, boop, chime, clank, click, crash, drop, footsteps, glassline, groove, inhale, knock, lub-dub, ping, plink, riser, snare-roll, swish, thud-drop, thump, ticks | all start **115-150 ms in** (drop 350, riser 320). Sharp hits land at 120-130 ms (ball-bounce, ball-hit, boop, clank, click, lub-dub, plink, thud-drop). Later peaks: boom 165, chime 150, swish 305, crash 400, footsteps 405, glassline 350, knock 575, thump 590, ping 630, groove 645, drop 1025, snare-roll 1065, riser 1265 |
| 7 wav, sharp | ding, hit, pop, stamp, step, thock, tick | hit at 0-5 ms: fine |
| 4 wav, swells | whoosh 265, rise 275, roll 185, swipe 155 | the peak is that many ms after the file starts |

**Why:** `Content/motion/tools/sounds.py` sets `LEAD = 0.10` ("every sample starts this far in") and the mp3 encoder adds a little more. The kit's
`sfx_html()` (`kit/kit.py` lines 80-93) puts the file's first sample on the event, with no allowance for either.
**Effect:** a menu sound lands about **4 frames after** the picture event (30 fps). A swell lands 5-8 frames late. A build-up (riser, drop, snare roll)
has to START 1.0-1.3 s before the event, and nothing in the code does that. To my knowledge no shipped render used the mp3 sounds yet (the showreels and
HCTV3230 cue the 11 wav sounds); they were wired in earlier on 2026-10-01.
**Fix:** store per sound a `lead` (seconds from the file start to its hit) and an `align` (`onset` for sharp sounds, `peak` for swells and build-ups) in
`kit/menu/menu.json`; `sfx_html()` then writes `data-start = E(t) - lead` (when that is below 0, use `data-media-start` instead). Check: render a 5 s test
and measure the hit in the output audio, it should be within 20 ms of the event.
**Sources agree on the principle (align the hit, not the file start) and differ by up to 50 ms on the numbers:** Hyperframes' own
`creator-editing-recipes.md` line 385 [A], haidrrrry (whoosh starts 2-3 frames before the landing), fernandokaraka ("80 ms of dead air is 80 ms late"),
adriiita (whoosh 10-20 ms before the move, impact on the hit frame), SeeCut (whoosh 80 ms early). Start with the hit on the frame and the whoosh peak on
the cut, then tune by ear.

### 2.2 `hyperframes check` guards the caption band, but only with dense sampling
`hyperframes check <public> --caption-zone "x0=0;y0=.12;x1=1;y1=.245;severity=error;seek=<fractions of the duration>"` runs fully offline on our pages:
**46 s for the 80 s HCTV3230 reel** (`C:\Users\Amir\Videos\Reels\hctv3230\v1\public`). The GPU probe says hardware capture (AMD RX 9070 XT).
- With 9 sample points it saw nothing in the band. **With 79 points (one per second) it caught `اسپرینت` at 41.4-42.2 s and `استارت` at 42.4 s**: the BOXES of the two
  behind-him words dip 30-110 px into the bottom of the band while they scale in (the word box starts at y 440 and enters at 1.6x scale; at rest it sits just below the band).
  **Correction (2026-10-01 night):** I first wrote that these words "sit in his caption band", which overstated it: they graze its lower edge for a moment, they are not on his captions.
  So generate the seek list from the reel's length, about one per 0.5 s.
- The same run reported, all deliberate in that reel: the final stamp words stacked on each other (78.2-80.35 s, an error), the court numbers hidden under
  the court (32-35 s), the ghost numeral at 1.5:1 contrast, a duplicated `<img>`, an audio slot 0.13 s longer than its file.
- So it reads Farsi and works, and the kit will need `data-layout-allow-overlap`, `data-layout-allow-occlusion` and `data-layout-allow-caption-zone` on
  the blocks that layer on purpose, so only an accident fails. Any layout error gives exit code 1.
- Also in `check`: `--frame-check` (text outside the frame), `--at-transitions`, and `*.motion.json` assertions (`appearsBy`, `before`, `staysInFrame`,
  `keepsMoving`) [A]. A lint error silently skips the layout and contrast audits (hyperframes-core SKILL.md:70) [A].

### 2.3 Safe zones: Meta's own number against our rule
Meta's Ads Guide for Reels ads (`https://www.facebook.com/business/ads-guide/update/video/instagram-reels`, fetched 2026-10-01) says to leave at least
14% of the top, 35% of the bottom and 6% of each side free of text, logos and other important elements. At 1080x1920: **top 269 px, bottom 672 px, sides
65 px: a free box of x 65-1015, y 269-1248.** What covers the rest: the profile icon, the call-to-action button, and taller phones crop the edges.
- **Ours:** the top band y 230-470 is his caption band and matches the 12-14% (fine). Our "read zone y 250-1600" and the default overlay zone (chest and
  desk, about y 1100-1500) reach below y 1248, which is inside Meta's 35% **for ads**.
- Others: Hyperframes' talking-head skill puts cards at y 1280-1844 with a keep-out below 1594 [A]; iart-ai top 120 / bottom 320 / right 120;
  charlie947 keeps reel content in y 360-1420; Remotion's skill wants 100 px top and bottom [A].
- **No official figure for organic Reels was found [U].** Organic Reels have no call-to-action bar, so the real bottom guard is probably lighter than 35%.
- **RESULT (the phone test, 2026-10-01 night, section 14.9): the guess was right and the right edge was missed.** Measured on his iPhone: nothing is covered from y 195 to y 1165; the profile row starts at
  y 1587 (our old y 1600 was right; Meta's 1248 is far too cautious for normal reels); the top bar ends at y 190; 52-54 px are cropped off each side; **and the like, comment, repost and share buttons cover
  x 920-993 from y 1171 to y 1770, which no source had told us.**
- ~~**Proposal (needs his phone, 5 minutes):**~~ done: I built a 10 s test card with rulers and lines at Meta's figures and our old rule, he opened it on his phone and sent a screenshot.
- [U, third party] The profile grid is 3:4 since 2025, so a 1080x1920 cover is centre-cropped to 1080x1440 (y 240-1680 kept).

## 5. Craft numbers worth copying (30 fps) and where they clash with his look
- **Timing:** enter 12-18 frames, exit 8-12 (an exit is 55-67% of its entrance). Ease-out on the way in, ease-in on the way out, linear only for loops.
  Hold at least 1 s after information settles. Text on screen at least 5/6 s (Netflix subtitle floor) and about 0.6 s per word reads it twice. A graphic
  that stays static past 2-3 s starts to read as a slide.
- **Stagger:** words 3 frames, cards 4-5, blocks 6 (40-70 ms for words).
- **Transitions:** only at turning points (hook, reveal, proof, call to action), hard cuts between; never two at one seam; one technique per edit; a
  black card at most twice per 30 s.
- **Caps per reel, to write on each BLOCKS.md card:** keyword pop 3, slam 2 (with a 45 frame hold), wipes 2, whip 2, pushes 4 in a row, full-frame
  impacts 3 and at least 16 beats apart.
- **Number block (talkcraft, shotcraft):** the slab lands (0.24 s) before the digits grow (0.28 s, `back.out` 1.7); the whole number before the decimals;
  tabular digits; hold 1.8 s; odometer digit stagger 7 frames, 16 frame deceleration, at most 6 digits.
- **Overshoot, calibrated:** stamp pop 2-4% over 5-9 frames, slam `back.out` 1.7 or below (1.0 subtle, 2.2 and up reads cartoon).
- **Behind-type:** at least 25% of the title hidden by him, type about 40% of the frame height, rise 0.6 s cubic-out from 70 px after a 0.4 s wait, a
  subtitle 0.35 s later, and check the hair edge of the matte; no letter-spacing in Farsi.
- **Chapter label, at most 2.5 s:** wipe in 0.3, number 0.4, name 0.35, hold 1.2, wipe out 0.3.
- **Wipes:** whip 6-8 frames at 300 px/frame or more with 8 px blur, the cut hidden mid-whip; iris 45 frames, circles concentric within 10 px; push 30
  frames; flood 0.3-0.35 s. Sub-frame motion blur (6-8 samples) only on wipes.
- **Type:** primary 84 px or more, secondary 44 px or more; one hero numeral per frame; a 390 px wide legibility check.
- **Finish:** on a talking head skip grade, grain, vignette and mesh (the repos that add them are making product films).
- **A card format worth copying (shotcraft, talkcraft):** name, one line, the job it does (hook, number, flow, conclusion, call to action), energy
  (low, mid, high), duration, a per-reel cap, exclusive A/B/C variants, a parameter table with "too low" and "too high" notes (keyword pop 1.3x is weak,
  above 1.9x it turns comic), known pitfalls, a "does not do" line, the sound pairing, and "validated on real footage?".
- **Clashes to settle:** see section 10.

## 6. Cutting logic: ideas for `plan_to_clips.py` and the render gate
What the planner does now: it cuts only inside real silences, never across a word, pauses over about 0.4 s down to 0.18 s, with a fixed `--noise`.
What the best repos add (all local, no cloud):
1. **Floor-calibrated threshold** (cut-video, krusemedia, AIEV): noise floor (10th percentile of the RMS) plus 6 dB instead of a fixed -35 dB; if speech
   minus floor is under 8 dB, energy detection is unusable and word gaps are used. (One repo found no pauses at -40 dB and 13 at -30 dB.) `--noise auto`.
2. **Graded gap compression** (cut-video): 0.25-1 s becomes 0.3 s, 1-3 s becomes 0.8 s, over 3 s becomes 0.5 s; keep rhetorical pauses of 0.15 s or
   more. Report the median cut, the percent cut and cuts per minute against its benchmarks (median cut 1.1-1.5 s, 40-60% cut).
3. **Envelope-refined edges** (hassancs91, krusemedia, ghost-editor): lead-in 0.15-0.18 s before the first energy jump, tail walked to the floor plus
   5-6 dB, 0.10-0.15 s of room, edges rounded inward to the frame grid. Whisper times are hints, not facts.
4. **A retake pass before the silence pass** (leadgenman, cut-video, hassancs91): normalise Farsi (yeh and kaf variants, half-space, digits), compare
   neighbouring phrases within 30-60 s, keep the LAST take, show the groups to Amir. Python's own `difflib` is enough; no install. A spoken slate word
   can supersede the previous run. Whisper often collapses retakes and drops fillers, so re-transcribe the isolated 5-6 s around a suspect seam.
5. **Stutter and boundary tells** (cut-video, reel-recut): flag a function word over 0.6 s, a kept word over 1 s, edge silence over 0.35 s, confidence under 0.7.
6. **One audio graph with fades** (krusemedia, video-use, AIEV): 10 ms fades at every cut (30 ms on a noisy seam), never cut audio and video apart
   (drift of 367-412 ms over 180 s measured). **[V] Ours already fade:** the planner puts a 20 ms fade on every real cut (`FADE = 0.02` in `plan_to_clips.py`, a volume lane on each audio
   clip). On the finished HCTV3230 all 9 cuts score 0.5x their surroundings in the new seam-click scan, a hard cut mid-speech scores 8.4x, so the scan can hear a click and ours have none.
7. **Zoom grammar and a hook** (ghost-editor, adriiita, SeeCut): alternate framing at kept cuts (zoom 1.07-1.12, 0.4 s push), a visual change every 2-4
   s but uneven; the hook gets a pattern interrupt and a sound cue within 0.5 s. (No repo gives speed-change rules for talking heads: ours are our own.)
8. **A layout lint on the DOM** (SeeCut, ghost-editor): tag elements, and at sample frames fail on overlap over 15%, the face covered over 8%, or any box
   in y 230-470. Hyperframes' `check` already does most of it (2.2); a face-box test needs OpenCV or YuNet [U].
9. **A sound plan from the graphics' hit frames** (ghost, SeeCut, hassancs91, jc-remotion): a pop on the frame, an impact on the hit, a whoosh early;
   levels against the voice's 95th percentile: UI -14 dB, whoosh -12, impact -8; 6-12 cues a minute, at least 2.5 s apart, one sound at most 35% of the
   cues, no low impacts on key words; `adelay` plus `amix normalize=0`, a 2:1 duck, a limiter, voice normalised first.
10. **A render gate** (ghost `qa.py`, krusemedia `video-qa`, hassancs91): size 1080x1920, duration within ±0.2 s, audio and video stream lengths equal,
    loudness and true peak, silence over 0.8 s inside speech, `freezedetect`, a seam-click scan, exit codes; plus Hyperframes lint and `check`.
11. **Seam sheets for Claude to look at** (video-use, naive-video-skill, krusemedia): per seam a ±1.5 s composite, a 24-frame contact sheet, a 5.5 s
    re-probe of a suspect seam, never re-whisper the whole render, at most 3 fix rounds, auto-fixes only within a whitelist (nudge up to 500 ms, gain up
    to 3 dB, fade 25-30 ms).
12. **One edit list, graphics bound to words** (chengfeng, krusemedia): remap graphic and sound times by code, never by re-transcribing; start each
    graphic within 0.2 s of its word's onset and never before it is said. (`E(...)` in our kit already does the remap.)
Typical numbers seen: whisper word ends overshoot into pauses by 0.1-0.4 s; Whisper times are off by ±100-300 ms, a forced aligner by 10-20 ms.

## 7. Farsi: word timing and on-screen text

### 7.1 Word timing (the pause smear)
**RESULT (2026-10-01 night, section 14): option 1 was tried and WORKS, and it replaced the earlier assumption that "our build already passes `--dtw`, so there is nothing to tune".**
whisper.cpp prints `dtw_token_timestamps is not supported with flash_attn - disabling` and flash attention is on by default, so Hyperframes' `--dtw` has never produced a DTW time on this PC
(every `t_dtw` is -1) and its parser reads the plain token `offsets` anyway. With `--no-flash-attn` DTW works, and clamping every word out of the silences does the rest. The default is now
`tools/word_times.py transcribe` (DTW + clamp). **The VAD file was fetched, verified and tried, and made the timing slightly WORSE on HCTV3230, so it is not used.**
Whisper's word times smear around pauses: the first word after a pause starts at the pause's start, and a list spoken with pauses can be 0.5 s off. Options, as planned:
1. **Clamp plus two whisper.cpp flags** (DONE, see above and section 14). Our `whisper-cli` has `--vad -vm <silero file>` (decode speech only), `--dtw large.v3 -ojf`
   (token times from the alignment heads) and `--prompt` (a glossary for misheard terms such as تمرین). Then clamp every word to the `silencedetect` map.
   Success was: words after a pause start within 60 ms of the audible onset. Measured: 23% before, 100% by the silence map and 85% by an independent acoustic check after.
2. **DROPPED 2026-10-02, never installed (he said to remove WhisperX if it is not necessary; it is not: section 14.8).** **WhisperX `align`** with its Persian model on text Amir has confirmed (1-2 h). `jonatasgrosman/wav2vec2-large-xlsr-53-persian` (Apache-2.0, 1.26 GB,
   character CTC, 30% WER and 7.4% CER on Common Voice 6.1) [V that WhisperX maps `fa` to it]. CPU-only works [A]. Pain: it needs a Python below 3.14 and
   pins torch. `load_audio` shells out to ffmpeg [A]. `align()` takes any `{text,start,end}` segments, so our corrected text works, one segment per speech
   run. The model's 67-token vocabulary has no half-space and no digits: spell numbers out; a word with no vocabulary characters gets no time.
   Success: 90% of 30 hand-checked word starts within 50 ms, none beyond 120 ms, and cuts padded 50/80 ms clip no consonant.
3. **Fallbacks:** `ctc-forced-aligner` (BSD-2 code; its default MMS model is CC-BY-NC, so pass the Apache Persian model; sdist-only with C++, so
   Windows may need a compiler). `stable-ts align` (MIT, archived 2026-05, Whisper cross-attention on confirmed text, an A/B partner).
4. **Not worth it:** Montreal Forced Aligner (no Persian model), whisper-timestamped (AGPL), Parakeet (no Farsi), Qwen3-ForcedAligner (no Persian),
   NeMo and Meta Omnilingual (heavy, not native Windows), a fine-tuned `vhdm/whisper-large-fa-v1` (timestamp quality unknown).
Calibration: even a good aligner is about ±100 ms on speech; keep padding cuts and clamp to silence.

### 7.2 Farsi on screen: guards that every source confirms, and the one new idea
- Never `<html dir>` (lint `html_dir_attribute_breaks_render`; Hyperframes #1934: previews fine, renders a blank video). Keep `lang="fa"` on `<html>`
  and put `direction:rtl` on a wrapper. We already follow this.
- Words as `inline-block` split on whitespace only (the half-space is not whitespace, so می‌روم stays one unit); reveal by mask or `clip-path` on intact
  text; static word spans emitted from the corrected transcript, no runtime SplitText. `letter-spacing:0`, `text-transform:none` always (registry blocks use
  negative tracking and uppercase). A Latin word or number between Persian words: isolate the LTR run in one `unicode-bidi:isolate` unit [A].
- **Ban list (per-letter blocks):** `bottom-up-letters`, `top-down-letters`, `char-slam-explode`, `scramble-reveal`, `variable-font-flex`, `weight-wave`,
  `typewriter`, `matrix-decode`, `kinetic-chars`, `per-word-rise` in `char` mode, `tracking-in`. Word-level and safe: `headline-slam`, `per-word-rise`
  (`split=word`), `staggered-fade-up`, `blur-in`.
- Fonts: Vazirmatn as local `@font-face`. The Google Fonts fallback is subset from static text, warns in lint and fails on cloud renders (#3496, #3583). None
  of the 18 bundled Hyperframes fonts is Arabic-script.
- **The one new idea (untested):** per-letter OPACITY on plain inline spans may keep the joins in Chrome (transforms do nothing on inline spans, and
  `inline-block` isolates letters). GSAP's `splitArabicText` re-joins with U+200D, but an agent read its non-joiner list as missing ژ and ignoring the
  half-space [A], so do not assume it handles Persian. A 10 minute local test (no download) would settle it, and it would only open a letter-fade, not a
  typewriter. Low priority: he has never asked for letter-level type.
- No repo, registry block, Remotion doc or GSAP helper does Persian animation. That is the reason to keep our own word-level Farsi blocks.

### 14.4 The planner (A4)
- **`--noise auto`.** My first attempt (the 10th percentile of the loudest sample of each 50 ms window, plus 6 dB) was WRONG: with fast speech the quietest tenth sits in the valleys between words and it
  chose -26.8 dB for a room whose floor is -41. The level histogram of HCTV3230 shows a clear room cluster at -42 dB, a gap, then speech from -36, so it now takes **the lowest dense cluster plus 6 dB**:
  raw HCTV3230 -41 dB floor, threshold -35 (the old default was right for that clip); the cleaned `voice.m4a` (10 dB louder) -33 floor, threshold -27 (a fixed number would be wrong there); `IMG_4315`
  -39, threshold -33. With no clear floor it says so and uses -35.
- `--graded` (1-3 s pauses keep 0.30 s, longer keep 0.50 s), and stats printed with every proposal (median cut, share of the speech stretch removed, cuts a minute).
- **`retakes`**: finds the same opening words again within 45 s, normalising Farsi letters, half-spaces and digits (`difflib`, no install). An injected restart (5 words, 0.7 s pause, the full line, with Arabic yeh

### 14.5 Word times, the VAD file and a finding about `--dtw` (A4, B1)
- **The VAD file:** `ggml-silero-v5.1.2.bin`, 885,098 bytes, fetched from `huggingface.co/ggml-org/whisper-vad` to `C:\Users\Amir\tools\whisper.cpp\models\`, **sha256 29940d98d42b91fbd05ce489f3ecf7c72f0a42f027e4875919a28fb4c04ea2cf, identical
  to the one Hugging Face lists**, loads in our `whisper-cli` and runs (245 words from HCTV3230). **It is fine, and it did not help**: see the table.
- **The pause smear, measured** on HCTV3230 (13 pauses of 0.25 s or more at -35 dB): the next word starts INSIDE the pause by a median of 154 ms (worst 590) and only 23% start where the sound actually rises
  (an independent check: the energy in the 50 ms after the word's start against the 100 ms before it, 3x or more); 6 words sit entirely inside a pause.
- **The finding:** whisper.cpp prints `dtw_token_timestamps is not supported with flash_attn - disabling` and flash attention is on by default, so `hyperframes transcribe --dtw` has **never produced a DTW time on
  this PC** (every `t_dtw` is -1), and Hyperframes' parser reads the plain token `offsets` anyway. The research's claim "our build already uses `--dtw`, so there is nothing to tune" was wrong. With
  `--no-flash-attn` the DTW times exist (758 of 778 tokens; the run takes about 3 minutes instead of 2).
- **What the planner finds from each version of the transcript** (same audio, `--noise -35`, pauses cut at `--min 0.40` / 0.30 / 0.25):

| transcript | 0.40 | 0.30 | 0.25 | starts where the sound rises (3x) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Hyperframes today | **1** (0.3 s) | 2 (0.5 s) | 5 (0.8 s) | 23% |
| + clamp | 3 (1.1 s) | 5 (1.5 s) | 8 (1.7 s) | 85% |
| VAD + clamp | 2 (1.4 s) | 4 (1.7 s) | 4 (1.7 s) | 85% |
| flash attention off, offsets | 1 (0.3 s) | 3 (0.7 s) | 6 (0.9 s) | 23% |
| + clamp | 5 (2.0 s) | 9 (2.8 s) | 12 (3.1 s) | 85% |
| flash attention off, DTW times | 4 (1.8 s) | 7 (2.4 s) | 9 (2.5 s) | 54% |
| **DTW + clamp (the new default)** | **7** (3.4 s) | 10 (4.0 s) | 12 (4.2 s) | 77% |

  Reading it: the old pipeline found 1 pause where this finds 7, because Whisper's smeared and phantom words vetoed the pauses ("never cut across a word"). The clamp (pull every word out of the
  silences) is the main fix; DTW removes most phantom words (2 against 6) and gets 100% of starts within 60 ms of the pause end by the silence map; the VAD made it worse (10 phantom words, 77% within
  60 ms). One clip only, a fast, dense talker: a slower speaker or a noisier room is untested.
- **`tools/word_times.py transcribe`** is now step 4 of the workflow (DTW + clamp, `--noise auto`); `eval` scores any transcript; `clamp` repairs an old one.

### 14.10 The cut-out trial: RobustVideoMatting (2026-10-02)
He said "yes if you think faster cut out will help you, install". It does: the depth family is his taste, and the 12-minute wait (plus a second pass for the trim) was the slowest step before any depth reel.
**Installed, after his yes:** `onnxruntime` 1.30.0 with flatbuffers 25.12.19 and protobuf 7.36.2 (pip, his Python 3.14, user site), and the model `rvm_mobilenetv3_fp32.onnx` (14,975,696 bytes, sha256 88d45312...cbbd2828,
the project's v1.0.0 release) in `C:\Users\Amir\tools\rvm\`. Nothing else: no OpenCV, no torch, no second Python.
- **How it runs** (`tools/rvm_cutout.py`, called by `tools/cutout.py` by default): decode, then the model at downsample ratio 0.25 (it sees 270 x 480 and refines at full size; a recurrent memory carries from frame to
  frame; the first frame is run 3 times to set it), then ONLY the alpha (1 byte a pixel) goes down a pipe into a second ffmpeg that reads the clip itself, erodes the alpha twice, feathers it (sigma 1), merges it with
  the clip's own pixels and encodes VP9 with alpha ONCE. The first version (scipy trim and RGBA packing in Python, all in turn) ran at 6.3 fps; moving the trim and merge into ffmpeg and reading and writing on threads gave 10.5 fps.
- **Speed (HCTV3230, 84.7 s, 2540 frames):** 249 s = **2.94 s of work per source second** (u2net: about 7 s, plus a 2 minute trim pass: 12 minutes). The model alone is 56 ms a frame on an idle CPU and about 90 ms while the encoders
  share the six cores. Finer settings cost more and showed nothing on his hair after the trim: ratio 0.375 = 4.3 s per second, 0.5 = 6.2 s per second (6 s excerpts, 1:1 hair crops side by side). The target of "2 s or less" was
  missed; "2-3 times faster" held (2.4 times).
- **The file:** 1080 x 1920 VP9, 30 fps, `alpha_mode=1`, 2540 frames = the source's 2540, 84.666 s, 107,884,688 bytes (u2net's: 91,827,045). Kept at `...\hctv3230\cutout\rvm\full_cut_t.webm` next to the u2net one, which stays: the shipped reel and showreels 2-4 were built on it.
- **Quality, every frame** (alpha area-averaged to 270 x 480, person = alpha above 0.5, against the u2net file; per-frame table in `...\cutout\rvm\fullcheck_vs_u2net.csv`): agreement IoU 0.984 on average, 0.950 at the 1st percentile,
  0.917 at worst (72.57 s). **Steadier:** the person's area changes by 27 px from one frame to the next against 94 px for u2net (3.5 times), its spread is 467 against 646 px, the soft edge's frame-to-frame jitter 0.053 against 0.060.
