## Cycle 01 — Iron Floor · 2026-09-03 · NEW

**The read**

25yo male, 78 kg, Tehran, full gym, semi-pro padel, on court 3-4 days/week, 3+ years gym
experience. His complaint verbatim (Farsi): *"سرعتم تو بازی به خصوص پاهام کمه"* — his speed
in the game, especially his legs, is lacking. Secondary soft goal added by Amir: he wants to
look better too, served by one optional 4th day.

**The diagnosis that drove everything.** A padel court is 10×20 m. There is no top-end speed
to lack. In a semi-pro racquet player, "my legs are slow" is a *braking* problem and a
force-floor problem wearing a speed complaint's clothes — he cannot decelerate hard enough,
so he arrives late and out of position and reads that as slow legs. His court volume has
already built his aerobic base, footwork patterns and repeated-effort tolerance. What it has
never built is the force to stop. That is what the gym is for, and it is what 10 of the 25
roadmap weeks are spent on before a single week is spent converting force to speed.

**Recovery ceiling (set before session count).** Sleep 7-8h but quality 5/10. Stress 3/10 —
genuinely low. Weekly life load HEAVY. Nutrition average and inconsistent. Read: not a stress
problem, a time-and-sleep-quality problem. Recovery is moderate, not poor. He is a
**concurrent athlete** carrying 6-8 sessions/week once padel is counted, and those 3-4 court
sessions are uncounted neural exposures that never appear in any log. Ceiling set at
**11-13 working sets per core day**, cost-weighted **44-50 units/week** across three core days
(hard ceiling 52), and a maximum of **two high-intent lower-body neural exposures per week in
the gym**. That last number governs the whole roadmap.

**Contraindications.** ACL reconstruction 3+ years ago, confirmed by Amir as fully cleared,
competing, asymptomatic. His own restriction: fine apart from very deep squats. Ruling: he is
**not a rehab case** and the programme must not read like one — plyometrics and COD ramp
normally. House knee-history rules still stand: reverse lunge over forward, no deep squatting,
lateral bounds ramp last (Cycle 4, never earlier). Graft type unknown — asked as a question,
deliberately *not* a gate, because nothing in Cycle 1 changes on the answer.

**Baseline.** Best lifts left blank on the intake form. Trust the bar over the résumé: assume
nothing, calibrate week 1 by RPE with the ceiling named as a number in the notes, and buy a
real filmed benchmark in week 2 before the heavy block is written.

**Structure.** 3 core days + 1 optional, decided by Amir. Day 1 squat force (PEAK, 17.0 units),
Day 2 hinge + vertical pull (MODERATE, 15.0), Day 3 single leg + landing (LOW, 14.5). The
alternative — 4 loaded days — loses to the concurrent load: at 3-4 court sessions a fourth
loaded gym day is a third uncounted neural exposure he cannot recover from with sleep quality
at 5/10.

**Decisions — PRIMARY LIFT SELECTIONS**

```
Knee-dominant  → Barbell Back Squat, parallel cap. TRANSFER OVERRIDES SFR here: leg press is
                 the higher-SFR option and it was rejected. He is a cleared semi-pro whose
                 restriction is DEPTH, not squatting, and the axial-loaded bilateral squat is
                 the biggest force-producing pattern available. Fallback Barbell Box Squat
                 (depth set mechanically).
Hinge          → Barbell Romanian Deadlift. Long-length hamstring is the standing tissue item
                 for all 25 weeks and this is its heaviest expression.
Single leg     → Dumbbell Reverse Lunge. House knee-history rule. Cycle 1 introduces and
                 calibrates the pattern; Cycle 2 (Split Steel) is where it goes heavy.
Landing tier 1 → Box Jump + Snap Down, both bilateral / vertical / planned / stick-landing.
                 Pogo Jump in prep for ankle stiffness. 60 ground contacts on each of Days 1
                 and 3, inside the 40-60 band.
Upper          → pull-biased per the racquet-athlete rule (Chest-Supported Row, Lat Pulldown
                 on core days). ALL pressing lives on the optional Day 4.
```
