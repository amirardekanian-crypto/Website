## Cycle 1 — mid-cycle adjustment · 2026-09-05 · elbow flare at competition

**The report.** 2026-09-04 Payam played a competition. Elbow pain after the FIRST match. A doctor
sprayed lidocaine on it and he kept playing. Amir's ask: "add some movement to strengthen his hand
to prevent further injuries." Legal under *Write once per cycle; adjust only on a report* — a report
exists. Design pass ran the standing multi-lens panel (tendinopathy · tennis kinetic chain · cardiac
safety veto · dose/time · house-rules lint, each attacked from four angles).

**The read.** The gym is almost certainly not the provocation. He is 11 days into a cycle carrying
**12 sets/week of heavy-slow wrist-extensor loading at 5 kg**, which is at or above the HSR dose in
the literature, and he got symptoms anyway. The spike was the court: first competitive match,
higher tensile rate at impact, more off-centre hits, longer rallies, a nervous grip. Cook/Purdam
reactive response in a previously quiet tendon.

**So the answer to "add more hand work" is NO, and it is a clinical no, not a time no.** Gripping
requires wrist-extensor co-contraction — grip strength is the standard *provocation* test for
lateral elbow tendinopathy. Loading grip in the week his ECRB was provoked would be loading the
injury. The straps-on-every-pull protocol is not a hole in his hand training, it **is** the elbow
protocol. Ball squeezes and grippers were both proposed by the panel and both killed.

⚠️ **The lidocaine is the part that matters most.** It blocks peripheral nerve endings for one to
two hours and does nothing to the tendon. He then kept playing with the only protective signal
switched off, on an athlete who already under-reports. So "he is alright" is a measurement taken
with the instrument off. The first reading worth anything is the next morning — which is why the
whole intervention is built on a next-morning gate, reusing the language his knee card already
teaches.

⚠️ **Stated deviation — the textbook isometric is DECLINED on cardiac grounds.** 5×45s at ~70% MVIC
is the best-evidenced analgesic protocol for this exact diagnosis. It is also a long submaximal
isometric with a fixed posture and a strong pull toward breath-holding, which is the category this
cycle already banned for carries and wall sits. "It's only forearm, it's a small muscle" is not a
defence: the handgrip pressor test uses a tiny muscle mass at 30% MVIC and still drives MAP up hard,
because the driver is occlusion and the metaboreflex, not muscle size. Recorded here so nobody
re-adds it in C2 thinking it was an oversight. Ceiling if a hold is ever needed: **≤10s, forearm
supported, counting out loud** — a coaching heuristic, not a measured threshold in this athlete, and
it does not move until the doctor's letter arrives.

**Decisions.**

```
Day 3 / Accessory — Cable Face Pull        3 → 2 sets            (buys ~2 min)
Day 3 / Accessory — Dumbbell Forearm Rotation  ADDED, last item  (2×12 ea, 2-0-2-0, RPE 6, rest 60)
Day 1 + Day 3 wrist extension              UNCHANGED prescription, load FROZEN via notes
Grip / hand-strength work                  REFUSED — it is the provocation test
```

**Why forearm rotation and not shoulder ER.** The kinetic-chain lens argued the glenohumeral
external rotators are the untouched link and should take the slot. Overruled for this change, and
the reasoning is worth keeping: (1) pronation/supination is the joint action at **zero** exposure
that the ECRB actually manages at ball impact, and it is what Amir asked for, honestly answered;
(2) that lens's own ER prescription was vetoed by the cardiac panel for a towel-held-against-the-ribs
cue that is a ~40s sustained adduction isometric per set; (3) **C3 "Uncoil" already owns rotational
and proximal work by design** — same accumulation-block logic that deferred transfer in C1.
→ **Cable External Rotation is the C2 Day 3 candidate**, replacing the face-pull slot properly.

⚠️ **Two cards were giving opposite instructions for the same joint.** "Week 2 Is Measuring Week"
told him to ramp the wrist work up to RPE 7 — and he is inside week 2 with an anaesthetised elbow.
Carved out. Also "The Forearm Work Is Not Optional" opened with *"Your tennis elbow is gone,"* which
as of 2026-09-04 is false and he knows it is false; that quietly costs the credibility of every
other card. Rewritten. Its never-cut rule also had to name what IS cuttable on Day 3 now that the
day carries two forearm items.

⚠️ **THE BIGGEST FINDING IS NOT THE ELBOW.** 24 of Payam's 25 RPE-carrying sets are logged **@10**
— on a program whose entire cardiac envelope is an RPE 7 ceiling. Verified against the whole
platform: he is at **96%, the highest of any athlete**, while everyone with 10+ sessions sits at
0–14% and most at 0–4% (pooya 1.0%, mhrn_zhr2 1.0%, pegah 0%, dela 0%). The high-rate cluster is
exclusively athletes under ~7 sessions (juan 60%, bardia 53%, setareh 44%). It reads as a
scale-comprehension problem that decays with exposure, **not** training to failure — his own sRPE
was 3/10 and 4/10 on those sessions and his logged note says he had far more reps in him. But it is
an inference, not proof, and on a cardiac restriction whose only guardrail is *stopping short* it
must be closed by **film, not by asking him**. Until it is, no load goes on anything on the basis of
these logs. Platform-level: the app never teaches the RPE scale before it asks for a number.

⚠️ **Day 2 is a real envelope breach and it is not the elbow.** Designed as the LOW day (RPE 6
throughout, cost-weighted 8.5 vs Day 1's 26.0). Returned **sRPE 9/10 and 576 AU, the highest session
load of the cycle**, with Machine Leg Extension ramped **40 → 54 → 81 kg inside three sets** on a
managed patellofemoral effusion. Unlike the per-set field, sRPE varies across his sessions (3, 9, 4),
so it is a field he engages with. Left unedited — outside the scope of what Amir asked for, and his
call to make.

**Standing rule added — the carries ban is about the MECHANISM, not the word.** Four questions, two
yeses and it is out: (1) is the muscle holding tension continuously beyond ~10s; (2) is it near max
or taken to fatigue; (3) is it a small muscle rather than a big one; (4) does the task make
breathing unnatural (locked trunk, arms overhead, no rep count to breathe against). Rules out
grippers, dead hangs, towel wrings, wrist rollers and the 30–45s isometric. Rules in anything
dynamic, moderate, rep-counted and exhaling. **Straps are part of this** — they stop a pulling set
turning into a grip-endurance test.

**Gates.** No load increase on either wrist slot until 72 h clear of any spray, gel or painkiller
**and** Amir has cleared a filmed set. If he uses the spray again at a tournament the clock restarts
from that date, not from when he says it stopped hurting. Progression trigger in the C1 table
amended: `3×10 pain-free at RPE 7` **AND three consecutive clear next-mornings.**

**Dated escalation — 27 September.** Still symptomatic then and he goes to a physio regardless of
what he says, on the same date as the cardiac letter deadline. Same-day referral triggers: pins and
needles or numbness in the forearm or hand (posterior interosseous entrapment mimics tennis elbow
and would make more forearm loading actively wrong), night pain, visible swelling, pain at the joint
line rather than over the epicondyle, sudden loss of grip on ordinary objects.

## Cycle 1 — Steel Breath · 2026-08-17 · NEW

**The read.**

Recovery capacity gates this athlete harder than his training age does: sleep 5/10, stress 6/10,
inconsistent fuelling, and 3–4 tennis sessions drawing on the same account. He was self-directing
5+ gym days on top of that. Ceiling set at ~10–12 hard sets/muscle/week, the low end of the
evidence range, not the 20 end. One compensation matters: the RPE 7 cap makes each set cheaper,
so **volume is this cycle's driver, not proximity to failure.**

Three medical conditions, all disclosed late and only verbally, none of them on the intake form.
He ticked "Return from Injury" and then declared no current and no past injury, which is the
profile of someone who under-reports because he believes reporting costs him training. The whole
communication design follows from that: the modification menu is in his hands before session one,
and every card says out loud that reporting buys a change and never a ban.

**The cardiac constraint governs everything, and it is read as a STRAIN rule, not a banned-exercise
list.** The doctor's sentence ("nothing too heavy off the floor") points at the Valsalva-driven
pressor response, and that response does not stop at the floor. So the envelope is about how every
set ends and how every rep breathes, from which selection falls out rather than being bolted on.
The naive safe option is inverted deliberately: light weight to failure is the *highest* blood
pressure protocol available, so the prescription is moderate load, moderate reps, stopped short,
generous rest.

Convenient consequence: for this athlete the highest-SFR choice IS the lowest-pressor choice,
because bracing demand is intrathoracic-pressure demand. Transfer is therefore deliberately
deferred to Uncoil (C3) per the accumulation-block principle, and nothing is lost by waiting.

Knee is a managed patellofemoral effusion under someone else's clinical care. Closed chain gated
by a mat stack, open chain restricted to 90–45° because open-chain stress is worst near terminal
extension. Elbow is a resolved tennis elbow treated prophylactically rather than avoidantly —
heavy-slow wrist-extensor loading with a number on it, because an exercise with a target gets done
and an exercise labelled prehab is the first thing cut when a session runs long.

**Decisions.**

```
PRIMARY LIFT SELECTIONS
Hip extension   → Single-Leg Hip Thrust (machine, or barbell from blocks)   [Standard 1]
Knee-dominant   → Dumbbell Reverse Lunge, mat-stack gated, straps           [Standard 3]
Horizontal pull → Single-Arm Chest-Supported Dumbbell Row, straps + neutral [Standard 2]
Vertical pull   → Lat Pulldown, neutral grip + straps
Elbow insurance → Wrist Extension, heavy-slow 3s/3s                         [Standard 4]
```

Forks Amir settled at the checkpoint: **one neutral-grip DB press** in C1 (not zero, not a machine
press) · **elbow work 2×/wk across two different movements** (the once-per-cycle rule forces
different movements) · **ramp-and-stop stays athlete-specific**, not appended to the principles file.

⚠️ **Stated deviation from COACHING-PRINCIPLES → Testing.** The house rule makes a baseline a filmed
AMRAP to technical failure. That is the most Valsalva-heavy thing that could go in this program, so
the baseline is **ramp-and-stop** instead: ramp in small jumps, stop at RPE 7 or the first rep that
slows, last completed set is the baseline, capped e1RM off an RIR chart. Same instrument, approached
from below. Costs a little precision, buys the whole envelope.
