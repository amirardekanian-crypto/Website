## Cycle 1 — Steel Breath · 2026-08-17 · NEW

**The read.**

Recovery capacity gates this athlete harder than his training age does: sleep 5/10, stress 6/10,
inconsistent fuelling, and 3–4 tennis sessions drawing on the same account. He was self-directing
5+ gym days on top of that. Ceiling set at ~10–12 hard sets/muscle/week, the low end of the
evidence range, not the 20 end. One compensation matters: the RPE 7 cap makes each set cheaper,
so **volume is this cycle's driver, not proximity to failure.**

Three medical conditions, all disclosed late and only verbally, none of them on the intake form.
He ticked "Return from Injury" and then declared no current and no past injury, which is the
profile of someone who under-reports because he believes reporting costs him training. The whole
communication design follows from that: the modification menu is in his hands before session one,
and every card says out loud that reporting buys a change and never a ban.

**The cardiac constraint governs everything, and it is read as a STRAIN rule, not a banned-exercise
list.** The doctor's sentence ("nothing too heavy off the floor") points at the Valsalva-driven
pressor response, and that response does not stop at the floor. So the envelope is about how every
set ends and how every rep breathes, from which selection falls out rather than being bolted on.
The naive safe option is inverted deliberately: light weight to failure is the *highest* blood
pressure protocol available, so the prescription is moderate load, moderate reps, stopped short,
generous rest.

Convenient consequence: for this athlete the highest-SFR choice IS the lowest-pressor choice,
because bracing demand is intrathoracic-pressure demand. Transfer is therefore deliberately
deferred to Uncoil (C3) per the accumulation-block principle, and nothing is lost by waiting.

Knee is a managed patellofemoral effusion under someone else's clinical care. Closed chain gated
by a mat stack, open chain restricted to 90–45° because open-chain stress is worst near terminal
extension. Elbow is a resolved tennis elbow treated prophylactically rather than avoidantly —
heavy-slow wrist-extensor loading with a number on it, because an exercise with a target gets done
and an exercise labelled prehab is the first thing cut when a session runs long.

**Decisions.**

```
PRIMARY LIFT SELECTIONS
Hip extension   → Single-Leg Hip Thrust (machine, or barbell from blocks)   [Standard 1]
Knee-dominant   → Dumbbell Reverse Lunge, mat-stack gated, straps           [Standard 3]
Horizontal pull → Single-Arm Chest-Supported Dumbbell Row, straps + neutral [Standard 2]
Vertical pull   → Lat Pulldown, neutral grip + straps
Elbow insurance → Wrist Extension, heavy-slow 3s/3s                         [Standard 4]
```

Forks Amir settled at the checkpoint: **one neutral-grip DB press** in C1 (not zero, not a machine
press) · **elbow work 2×/wk across two different movements** (the once-per-cycle rule forces
different movements) · **ramp-and-stop stays athlete-specific**, not appended to the principles file.

⚠️ **Stated deviation from COACHING-PRINCIPLES → Testing.** The house rule makes a baseline a filmed
AMRAP to technical failure. That is the most Valsalva-heavy thing that could go in this program, so
the baseline is **ramp-and-stop** instead: ramp in small jumps, stop at RPE 7 or the first rep that
slows, last completed set is the baseline, capped e1RM off an RIR chart. Same instrument, approached
from below. Costs a little precision, buys the whole envelope.

**Exercise Ledger Updates.** First cycle, so every working exercise enters `Active`. Box Step-Up and
Machine Hip Thrust enter `Available` (written in as fallbacks, not programmed). Five entries enter

carries, all straight-bar elbow flexion, and the quad isometric holds. None of those are rotation

Deload call: bodyweight down >1.5 kg across two weeks, **or** logged RPE drifting +1 on an unchanged
prescription for two straight weeks → cut cycle volume ~30% for a week. Week 5 is a planned back-off
regardless.

⚠️ **Depth is not the progression lever this cycle.** The lunge runs once a week and the gate needs
three clean sessions, so at most one mat-stack drop is possible (week 4 earliest) and zero is the
likely outcome. Do not read a flat depth at the C1 review as a failed progression — it is the design.

**e1RM.** None. No baselines exist until the week-2 ramp-and-stop session.
