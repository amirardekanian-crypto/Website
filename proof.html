What it actually is

A tracker you open once a day and tick. It takes about twenty seconds. What makes it stick is what happens underneath.

01

Every habit has its own level

Not one score for everything. You can be GRINDER 2 on water and ROOKIE 5 on training at the same time — which is usually the honest picture.

02

Ten ranks, fifty levels

ROOKIE through IMMORTAL, five levels each. Past fifty you start again carrying a star. There is no ceiling and nothing to buy.

03

Runs you won't want to break

Consecutive days on one habit build a run. Hit five, ten, twenty, thirty, sixty and each pays. Breaking one never takes a badge back.

You are not doing it alone

The part most trackers miss. There is a board with other people on it, and a wall where everyone gets one sentence a day — what broke you, what you nearly talked yourself out of, what actually worked.

It is not a feed and there is no scrolling. One line each, and it stands until midnight. I write one most days too.

"Legs went at rep 8. Finished anyway."
"Travel day. Got the steps in, nothing else."
"Slept nine hours and felt worse. Explain that."

What you track

Eight to start with. Switch off anything you would only lie about later — you can change all of it whenever you like.

Steps Sleep Water Food Mobility Breathing Supplements Workout — coached athletes only

That last one is honest rather than coy. The training habit is worth the most points of any of them, and it ticks itself when you finish a session in a programme I have written for you. Without one it stays locked — so a perfect day is something only my coached athletes can reach. Everything else is yours, free, for as long as you want it.
